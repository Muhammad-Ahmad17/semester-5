# QUESTION #1: Differentiate the Following [1.5 × 4 = 6 Marks]

## QUESTION TEXT

a) Micro economics vs. Macro economics
b) Price demand vs. Cross demand
c) Movement in Demand curve vs. a shift of demand curve
d) Market equilibrium vs. Market disequilibrium

---

# SOLUTION TO QUESTION #1

## Problem 1: Compact Discs

## (a) Microeconomics vs. Macroeconomics

| Aspect | Microeconomics | Macroeconomics |
|--------|---|---|
| **Definition** | Studies individual economic units (consumers, firms, industries) | Studies the economy as a whole (national income, employment, inflation) |
| **Scope** | Small scale - individual, firm, market level | Large scale - national, international economy |
| **Focus** | Price determination, consumer behavior, firm production decisions | GDP, unemployment rate, inflation, national income |
| **Analysis** | Partial equilibrium analysis | General equilibrium analysis |
| **Decision Maker** | Individual consumers and businesses | Government and central bank |
| **Example** | Why does the price of graphics cards change? | Why does inflation occur in the country? |

---

## (b) Price Demand vs. Cross Demand

| Factor | Price Demand | Cross Demand |
|--------|---|---|
| **Definition** | Relationship between price of a good and quantity demanded of that same good | Relationship between price of one good and quantity demanded of another related good |
| **Variable Changed** | Own price of the product changes | Price of related product changes |
| **Direction of Relationship** | **Inverse** - As price ↑, Qd ↓ (Downward sloping) | **Depends on relationship**: Substitutes → Direct; Complements → Inverse |
| **Elasticity** | Price Elasticity of Demand (PED) | Cross Price Elasticity (XED) |
| **Example** | Price of graphics card ↑ → Demand for graphics cards ↓ | Price of GPU drivers ↓ → Demand for graphics cards ↑ (complement) |

---

## (c) Movement in Demand Curve vs. Shift of Demand Curve

| Aspect | Movement Along Demand Curve | Shift of Demand Curve |
|--------|---|---|
| **Definition** | Change in quantity demanded due to change in **own price** only | Change in **entire demand** due to non-price factors |
| **Cause** | Price of the product changes | Income, preferences, related goods prices, expectations, number of buyers, etc. |
| **Visual Change** | Points move **up/down on the same curve** | **Entire curve moves** left or right |
| **Direction** | Upward (price ↓) or Downward (price ↑) along existing curve | Outward (increase) = Rightward; Inward (decrease) = Leftward |
| **Demand Changes?** | No, only quantity demanded changes | Yes, entire demand schedule changes |
| **Example** | Graphics card price ↑ from $250 → $300 = movement along curve | Income of students ↑ → demand for graphics cards increases = rightward shift |

---

## (d) Market Equilibrium vs. Market Disequilibrium

| Aspect | Market Equilibrium | Market Disequilibrium |
|--------|---|---|
| **Definition** | Point where quantity supplied equals quantity demanded at a specific price | Point where quantity supplied ≠ quantity demanded |
| **Price** | **Equilibrium price** - market-clearing price | Prices are either **too high** or **too low** |
| **Stability** | Stable, no tendency to change | Unstable, pressure to change |
| **Surplus/Shortage** | Neither surplus nor shortage | Either surplus (Qs > Qd) or shortage (Qd > Qs) |
| **Market Pressure** | No pressure to adjust | Pressure toward equilibrium (prices adjust) |
| **Example** | Price of graphics card = $300, Qs = Qd = 2,200 units | Price = $250, Qs = 1,500 but Qd = 2,600 (shortage exists) |
| **Tendency** | Static - stays at equilibrium | Dynamic - moves toward equilibrium |

# QUESTION #2: Material Selection (Aluminium vs Steel Casting) [4 Marks]

## Given Information

| Factor | Aluminium | Steel |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| Weight | 5 kg | 7 kg |
| Casting cost | Rs. 125/kg | Rs. 60/kg |
| Machining cost | Rs. 200/unit | Rs. 250/unit |
| **Excess weight penalty** | Rs. 2,500 per kg | Rs. 2,500 per kg |

---

## Solution

### Step 1: Calculate Casting Cost

**Aluminium:**
$$\text{Casting Cost}_{\text{Al}} = 5 \text{ kg} \times \text{Rs. } 125/\text{kg} = \text{Rs. } 625$$

**Steel:**
$$\text{Casting Cost}_{\text{Steel}} = 7 \text{ kg} \times \text{Rs. } 60/\text{kg} = \text{Rs. } 420$$

---

### Step 2: Calculate Machining Cost

**Aluminium:**
$$\text{Machining Cost}_{\text{Al}} = \text{Rs. } 200$$

**Steel:**
$$\text{Machining Cost}_{\text{Steel}} = \text{Rs. } 250$$

---

### Step 3: Calculate Weight Penalty

The excess weight is the difference compared to the lighter option (aluminium = 5 kg).

**Aluminium:**
$$\text{Excess Weight}_{\text{Al}} = 0 \text{ kg}$$
$$\text{Weight Penalty}_{\text{Al}} = 0 \times \text{Rs. } 2,500 = \text{Rs. } 0$$

**Steel:**
$$\text{Excess Weight}_{\text{Steel}} = 7 - 5 = 2 \text{ kg}$$
$$\text{Weight Penalty}_{\text{Steel}} = 2 \text{ kg} \times \text{Rs. } 2,500/\text{kg} = \text{Rs. } 5,000$$

---

### Step 4: Calculate Total Cost per Unit

**Aluminium Total Cost:**
$$\text{Total}_{\text{Al}} = 625 + 200 + 0 = \text{Rs. } 825$$

**Steel Total Cost:**
$$\text{Total}_{\text{Steel}} = 420 + 250 + 5,000 = \text{Rs. } 5,670$$

---

## Recommendation & Economic Advantage

$$\boxed{\text{ALUMINIUM SHOULD BE SPECIFIED}}$$

**Economic Advantage:**
$$\text{Savings} = \text{Total}_{\text{Steel}} - \text{Total}_{\text{Al}} = 5,670 - 825 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 4,845 \text{ per unit}}$$

**Justification:** Although aluminium has higher casting costs, the light weight eliminates the fuel consumption penalty, resulting in substantial savings of **Rs. 4,845 per unit**.

# QUESTION #3: Price Elasticity of Demand for Graphics Cards [4 + 2 = 6 Marks]

## Given Data

| Price ($) | Quantity (Students) | Quantity (Casual Users) |
|-----------|-----|-----|
| 200 | 3000 | 1000 |
| 250 | 2600 | 800 |
| 300 | 2200 | 600 |
| 350 | 1800 | 400 |

---

## Part (a): Price Elasticity from $250 → $300

**Midpoint Method Formula:**
$$E_p = \frac{\frac{\Delta Q}{\text{Average Q}}}{\frac{\Delta P}{\text{Average P}}} = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{\text{Average P}}{\text{Average Q}}$$

---

### (i) Computer Engineering Students

**Step 1: Identify initial and final quantities**
- Initial (P = $250): Q₁ = 2,600
- Final (P = $300): Q₂ = 2,200

**Step 2: Calculate average quantity**
$$\text{Average Q} = \frac{2,600 + 2,200}{2} = \frac{4,800}{2} = 2,400$$

**Step 3: Calculate change in quantity**
$$\Delta Q = 2,200 - 2,600 = -400$$

**Step 4: Identify prices and calculate average**
- Initial price: P₁ = $250
- Final price: P₂ = $300
- Change: ΔP = $50
$$\text{Average P} = \frac{250 + 300}{2} = \frac{550}{2} = 275$$

**Step 5: Calculate elasticity**
$$E_p = \frac{-400}{50} \times \frac{275}{2,400} = (-8) \times (0.1146) = -0.9167$$

$$\boxed{E_p \approx -0.92 \text{ (Inelastic)}}$$

**Interpretation:** A 1% increase in price leads to approximately 0.92% decrease in quantity demanded. Demand is **inelastic** - students are relatively price-insensitive (possibly due to necessity for studies).

---

### (ii) Casual Users

**Step 1: Identify quantities**
- Initial (P = $250): Q₁ = 800
- Final (P = $300): Q₂ = 600

**Step 2: Calculate average quantity**
$$\text{Average Q} = \frac{800 + 600}{2} = \frac{1,400}{2} = 700$$

**Step 3: Change in quantity**
$$\Delta Q = 600 - 800 = -200$$

**Step 4: Average price (same as before)**
$$\text{Average P} = 275$$
$$\Delta P = 50$$

**Step 5: Calculate elasticity**
$$E_p = \frac{-200}{50} \times \frac{275}{700} = (-4) \times (0.3929) = -1.5714$$

$$\boxed{E_p \approx -1.57 \text{ (Elastic)}}$$

**Interpretation:** A 1% increase in price leads to approximately 1.57% decrease in quantity demanded. Demand is **elastic** - casual users are very price-sensitive and will significantly reduce purchases if prices increase.

---

## Part (b): Why Different Elasticities?

### Computer Engineering Students (Inelastic, Ep = -0.92)
1. **Necessity:** Graphics cards are essential for engineering coursework and projects
2. **Educational Value:** Not easily postponable - needed for semester work
3. **Budget Allocation:** Educational expenses are prioritized in their budget
4. **Limited Substitutes:** Cannot easily substitute with alternative products
5. **Long-term Investment:** Consider it as investment in education, not discretionary spending

### Casual Users (Elastic, Ep = -1.57)
1. **Discretionary Spending:** Gaming/casual use is optional, not essential
2. **Many Substitutes:** Can use older cards, rent time at internet cafes, play less demanding games
3. **Price-Sensitive:** Budget-constrained; easily defer purchases or reduce quantity
4. **Flexible Timing:** Can wait for price drops or sales
5. **Luxury Good:** Not a necessity; easily postponed or eliminated

### Summary
- **Students**: Inelastic → **essential good** with limited alternatives
- **Casual Users**: Elastic → **luxury good** with many alternatives and discretionary nature

# QUESTION #4A: Retirement Savings with Geometric Series [4 Marks]

## Given Information

- **Deposit frequency:** Annual, at the **end** of each year
- **Initial deposit (Year 1):** Rs. 8,500
- **Annual increase:** Rs. 500 per year
- **Time period:** 10 years
- **Interest rate:** 15% per annum
- **Required:** Find total amount at end of Year 10

---

## Deposit Schedule

| Year | Annual Deposit |
|------|-----------------|
| 1 | Rs. 8,500 |
| 2 | Rs. 9,000 |
| 3 | Rs. 9,500 |
| 4 | Rs. 10,000 |
| 5 | Rs. 10,500 |
| 6 | Rs. 11,000 |
| 7 | Rs. 11,500 |
| 8 | Rs. 12,000 |
| 9 | Rs. 12,500 |
| 10 | Rs. 13,000 |

---

## Solution Method: Arithmetic Gradient Series

This is an **Arithmetic Gradient Future Worth** problem.

**Formula for Future Value of Arithmetic Gradient:**

$$F = A \times \frac{(1+i)^n - 1}{i} + G \times \left[ \frac{(1+i)^n - 1}{i} - n \right] \times \frac{1}{i}$$

Where:
- A = Base annuity = Rs. 8,500
- G = Gradient (annual increase) = Rs. 500
- i = Interest rate = 15% = 0.15
- n = Number of years = 10

---

## Step 1: Calculate the Annual Amount Component

$$F_A = A \times \frac{(1+i)^n - 1}{i}$$

$$F_A = 8,500 \times \frac{(1.15)^{10} - 1}{0.15}$$

**Calculate (1.15)¹⁰:**
$$(1.15)^{10} = 4.0456$$

$$F_A = 8,500 \times \frac{4.0456 - 1}{0.15} = 8,500 \times \frac{3.0456}{0.15}$$

$$F_A = 8,500 \times 20.304 = \text{Rs. } 172,584$$

---

## Step 2: Calculate the Gradient Component

$$F_G = G \times \left[ \frac{(1+i)^n - 1}{i} - n \right] \times \frac{1}{i}$$

$$F_G = 500 \times \left[ \frac{3.0456}{0.15} - 10 \right] \times \frac{1}{0.15}$$

$$F_G = 500 \times \left[ 20.304 - 10 \right] \times 6.667$$

$$F_G = 500 \times 10.304 \times 6.667 = 500 \times 68.693$$

$$F_G = \text{Rs. } 34,347$$

---

## Step 3: Calculate Total Future Value

$$F_{\text{Total}} = F_A + F_G = 172,584 + 34,347$$

$$\boxed{F_{\text{Total}} = \text{Rs. } 206,931}$$

---

## Verification (Alternative Method: Individual Deposit Accumulation)

Each deposit accumulates to the end of Year 10:

- Year 1 deposit: Rs. 8,500 × (1.15)⁹ = Rs. 8,500 × 3.5179 = Rs. 29,902
- Year 2 deposit: Rs. 9,000 × (1.15)⁸ = Rs. 9,000 × 3.0590 = Rs. 27,531
- Year 3 deposit: Rs. 9,500 × (1.15)⁷ = Rs. 9,500 × 2.6600 = Rs. 25,270
- Year 4 deposit: Rs. 10,000 × (1.15)⁶ = Rs. 10,000 × 2.3131 = Rs. 23,131
- Year 5 deposit: Rs. 10,500 × (1.15)⁵ = Rs. 10,500 × 2.0114 = Rs. 21,120
- Year 6 deposit: Rs. 11,000 × (1.15)⁴ = Rs. 11,000 × 1.7490 = Rs. 19,239
- Year 7 deposit: Rs. 11,500 × (1.15)³ = Rs. 11,500 × 1.5209 = Rs. 17,490
- Year 8 deposit: Rs. 12,000 × (1.15)² = Rs. 12,000 × 1.3225 = Rs. 15,870
- Year 9 deposit: Rs. 12,500 × (1.15)¹ = Rs. 12,500 × 1.15 = Rs. 14,375
- Year 10 deposit: Rs. 13,000 × (1.15)⁰ = Rs. 13,000 × 1 = Rs. 13,000

**Total ≈ Rs. 207,928** (slight variation due to rounding)

✓ **Verified!**

# QUESTION #4B: Machine Selection Using Future Worth Method [5 Marks]

## Given Information

| Particulars | Machine A | Machine B |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Initial Cost** | Rs. 4,00,000 | Rs. 8,00,000 |
| **Useful Life** | 5 years | 5 years |
| **Salvage Value** | Rs. 2,00,000 | Rs. 5,50,000 |
| **Annual Maintenance Cost** | Rs. 40,000 | Rs. 0 |
| **Interest Rate** | 15% | 15% |

---

## Solution: Future Worth Method

**Future Worth = Negative of Initial Cost (in FW terms) + Future Value of Salvage - Future Value of Operating Costs**

### Formula
$$FW = -P(1+i)^n + S - \sum (\text{Annual Costs}) \times (1+i)^n \text{ terms}$$

Or more directly:
$$FW = -P(1+i)^n + S - \text{PV of Costs} \times (1+i)^n$$

---

## MACHINE A Analysis

### Step 1: Future Value of Initial Cost
$$FV_{\text{Cost, A}} = -P(1+i)^n = -4,00,000 \times (1.15)^5$$

Calculate $(1.15)^5$:
$$(1.15)^5 = 2.0114$$

$$FV_{\text{Cost, A}} = -4,00,000 \times 2.0114 = \text{Rs. } -8,04,560$$

---

### Step 2: Future Value of Salvage Value
$$FV_{\text{Salvage, A}} = S = \text{Rs. } +2,00,000$$

(Already at end of Year 5, no compounding needed)

---

### Step 3: Future Value of Annual Maintenance Costs

Annual maintenance = Rs. 40,000 per year for 5 years

$$FV_{\text{Maintenance, A}} = -40,000 \times \frac{(1+i)^n - 1}{i}$$

$$FV_{\text{Maintenance, A}} = -40,000 \times \frac{(1.15)^5 - 1}{0.15}$$

$$FV_{\text{Maintenance, A}} = -40,000 \times \frac{2.0114 - 1}{0.15} = -40,000 \times \frac{1.0114}{0.15}$$

$$FV_{\text{Maintenance, A}} = -40,000 \times 6.7427 = \text{Rs. } -2,69,708$$

---

### Step 4: Total Future Worth for Machine A

$$FW_A = FV_{\text{Cost}} + FV_{\text{Salvage}} + FV_{\text{Maintenance}}$$

$$FW_A = -8,04,560 + 2,00,000 - 2,69,708$$

$$\boxed{FW_A = \text{Rs. } -8,74,268}$$

---

## MACHINE B Analysis

### Step 1: Future Value of Initial Cost
$$FV_{\text{Cost, B}} = -8,00,000 \times (1.15)^5 = -8,00,000 \times 2.0114$$

$$FV_{\text{Cost, B}} = \text{Rs. } -16,09,120$$

---

### Step 2: Future Value of Salvage Value
$$FV_{\text{Salvage, B}} = \text{Rs. } +5,50,000$$

---

### Step 3: Future Value of Annual Maintenance Costs
$$FV_{\text{Maintenance, B}} = 0$$ (No maintenance costs)

---

### Step 4: Total Future Worth for Machine B

$$FW_B = -16,09,120 + 5,50,000 + 0$$

$$\boxed{FW_B = \text{Rs. } -10,59,120}$$

---

## Comparison & Recommendation

| Machine | Initial Cost | Maintenance (FW) | Salvage | Net FW |
|---------|--------------|------------------|---------|---------|
| **A** | Rs. -8,04,560 | Rs. -2,69,708 | Rs. 2,00,000 | **Rs. -8,74,268** |
| **B** | Rs. -16,09,120 | Rs. 0 | Rs. 5,50,000 | **Rs. -10,59,120** |

$$\boxed{\text{MACHINE A SHOULD BE SELECTED}}$$

**Economic Advantage:**
$$\text{Savings} = |FW_B| - |FW_A| = 10,59,120 - 8,74,268 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 1,84,852}$$

---

## Justification

Although Machine B has a higher salvage value (Rs. 5,50,000 vs Rs. 2,00,000), its **initial cost is double** (Rs. 8,00,000 vs Rs. 4,00,000), and this higher initial investment does not justify the additional expense. 

Machine A's lower initial cost combined with modest but manageable maintenance costs makes it the **economically superior choice**, saving approximately **Rs. 1,84,852 in present worth terms** over the 5-year period.

The salvage value benefit of Machine B is insufficient to offset its doubled initial capital requirement.
