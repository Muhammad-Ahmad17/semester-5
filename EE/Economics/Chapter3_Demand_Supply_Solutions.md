## 📉 1. DEMAND

### Definition:
**Demand** = Quantity of a commodity consumers are willing and able to purchase at a certain price during a specific time period

**Formula:**
$$\boxed{Q_d = f(P)}$$

**Where:**
| Symbol | Full Form | Unit | Description |
|--------|-----------|------|-------------|
| Qd | Quantity Demanded | units | Amount buyers want to purchase |
| P | Price | Rs./unit, $/unit | Price of the commodity |
| f( ) | Function of | - | Qd depends on P |

### Essential Requirements for Demand:
1. **Will** to purchase (desire)
2. **Power** to purchase (money/income)
3. **Price** consideration

---

## 📊 2. LAW OF DEMAND

### Statement:
**"There is an INVERSE relationship between price and quantity demanded, other things remaining constant"**

### Formula Expression:
$$\boxed{\begin{cases}
P \uparrow \Rightarrow Q_d \downarrow \\
P \downarrow \Rightarrow Q_d \uparrow
\end{cases}}$$

**Where:**
- ↑ = Increase
- ↓ = Decrease
- ⇒ = Leads to/Results in

### When to Use:
✓ Predicting consumer behavior with price changes  
✓ Pricing strategy decisions  
✓ Market demand analysis

### Example:
**Statement:** *"Bread price increases from $2 to $3. Demand falls from 100 to 70 loaves. Verify law of demand."*

```
Price: $2 → $3 (↑)
Quantity Demanded: 100 → 70 (↓)
P↑ and Qd↓ → Inverse relationship ✓
Law of Demand verified
```

---

## 📈 3. SUPPLY

### Definition:
**Supply** = Quantity of commodity firms are willing to produce and offer for sale at a given price during a specific time period

**Formula:**
$$\boxed{Q_s = f(P)}$$

**Where:**
| Symbol | Full Form | Unit | Description |
|--------|-----------|------|-------------|
| Qs | Quantity Supplied | units | Amount sellers want to sell |
| P | Price | Rs./unit, $/unit | Price of the commodity |

---

## 📊 4. LAW OF SUPPLY

### Statement:
**"There is a DIRECT relationship between price and quantity supplied, other things remaining constant"**

### Formula Expression:
$$\boxed{\begin{cases}
P \uparrow \Rightarrow Q_s \uparrow \\
P \downarrow \Rightarrow Q_s \downarrow
\end{cases}}$$

### When to Use:
✓ Predicting producer behavior  
✓ Analyzing supply responses to price changes  
✓ Market supply forecasting

### Example:
**Statement:** *"Wheat price increases from Rs. 20 to Rs. 25 per kg. Farmers increase supply from 500 kg to 700 kg. Verify law of supply."*

```
Price: Rs. 20 → Rs. 25 (↑)
Quantity Supplied: 500 → 700 (↑)
P↑ and Qs↑ → Direct relationship ✓
Law of Supply verified
```

---

## ⚖️ 5. MARKET EQUILIBRIUM

### Equilibrium Condition:
$$\boxed{Q_d = Q_s}$$

**Where:**
| Symbol | Full Form | Description |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Qd | Quantity Demanded | What consumers want to buy |
| Qs | Quantity Supplied | What producers want to sell |
| Pe | Equilibrium Price | Price where Qd = Qs |
| Qe | Equilibrium Quantity | Quantity where Qd = Qs |

### Definition:
**Equilibrium** = Market condition where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied
- No tendency for price to change
- No shortage or surplus

### When to Use:
✓ Finding market-clearing price  
✓ Analyzing stable market conditions  
✓ Predicting price movements

### Example Problem:
**Statement:** *"Demand: Qd = 100 - 2P. Supply: Qs = 20 + 3P. Find equilibrium price and quantity."*

**Solution:**
```
At equilibrium: Qd = Qs
100 - 2P = 20 + 3P
100 - 20 = 3P + 2P
80 = 5P
P = 16 ← Equilibrium Price (Pe)

Qe = 100 - 2(16) = 100 - 32 = 68 units
OR
Qe = 20 + 3(16) = 20 + 48 = 68 units ✓

Equilibrium: Pe = $16, Qe = 68 units
```

## 📊 6. MARKET DISEQUILIBRIA

### A. Excess Demand (Shortage)

**Condition:**
$$\boxed{Q_d > Q_s \Rightarrow \text{Price} \uparrow}$$

**Where:**
| Term | Full Form | Description |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| Excess Demand | Shortage | Consumers want more than available |
| Qd > Qs | Demand exceeds Supply | Not enough goods for buyers |

**Market Response:** Price **rises** until equilibrium restored

### Example:
**Statement:** *"At P = $10, Qd = 100, Qs = 60. Identify the situation."*

```
Qd = 100 > Qs = 60
Excess Demand = 100 - 60 = 40 units (Shortage)
Price will rise to eliminate shortage ↑
```

---

### B. Excess Supply (Surplus)

**Condition:**
$$\boxed{Q_s > Q_d \Rightarrow \text{Price} \downarrow}$$

**Where:**
| Term | Full Form | Description |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| Excess Supply | Surplus | Producers have more than consumers want |
| Qs > Qd | Supply exceeds Demand | Too many goods unsold |

**Market Response:** Price **falls** until equilibrium restored

### Example:
**Statement:** *"At P = $20, Qd = 50, Qs = 90. Identify the situation."*

```
Qs = 90 > Qd = 50
Excess Supply = 90 - 50 = 40 units (Surplus)
Price will fall to eliminate surplus ↓
```

## 🔄 7. CHANGES IN DEMAND

### A. Movement Along Demand Curve (Change in Quantity Demanded)

**Cause:** Change in **price of the good itself**

**Types:**

#### Extension of Demand
$$\boxed{P \downarrow \Rightarrow Q_d \uparrow \text{ (move down the curve)}}$$

**Example:** *"Price falls from $10 to $8, demand increases from 50 to 70 units"*

#### Contraction of Demand
$$\boxed{P \uparrow \Rightarrow Q_d \downarrow \text{ (move up the curve)}}$$

**Example:** *"Price rises from $10 to $12, demand decreases from 50 to 30 units"*

---

### B. Shift of Demand Curve (Change in Demand)

**Cause:** Change in **factors other than own price**

**Demand Shifters:**
1. **Consumer Income** (Y)
2. **Prices of Related Goods** (substitute/complement prices)
3. **Tastes and Preferences**
4. **Number of Consumers**
5. **Expectations** (future prices/income)

#### Increase in Demand
$$\boxed{\text{Demand Curve shifts RIGHT} \Rightarrow \text{At same P, higher } Q_d}$$

**Causes:**
- Income ↑ (for normal goods)
- Price of substitute ↑
- Price of complement ↓
- Favorable change in tastes

#### Decrease in Demand
$$\boxed{\text{Demand Curve shifts LEFT} \Rightarrow \text{At same P, lower } Q_d}$$

**Causes:**
- Income ↓ (for normal goods)
- Price of substitute ↓
- Price of complement ↑
- Unfavorable change in tastes

---

## 🔄 8. CHANGES IN SUPPLY

### A. Movement Along Supply Curve (Change in Quantity Supplied)

**Cause:** Change in **price of the good itself**

**Types:**

#### Expansion of Supply
$$\boxed{P \uparrow \Rightarrow Q_s \uparrow \text{ (move up the curve)}}$$

**Example:** *"Price rises from Rs. 20 to Rs. 25, supply increases from 100 to 150 units"*

#### Contraction of Supply
$$\boxed{P \downarrow \Rightarrow Q_s \downarrow \text{ (move down the curve)}}$$

**Example:** *"Price falls from Rs. 20 to Rs. 15, supply decreases from 100 to 70 units"*

---

### B. Shift of Supply Curve (Change in Supply)

**Cause:** Change in **factors other than own price**

**Supply Shifters:**
1. **Technology** (production methods)
2. **Cost of Production** (input prices)
3. **Government Policy** (taxes, subsidies)
4. **Prices of Other Goods** (alternative products firm could make)
5. **Number of Sellers**
6. **Expectations** (future prices)

#### Increase in Supply
$$\boxed{\text{Supply Curve shifts RIGHT} \Rightarrow \text{At same P, higher } Q_s}$$

**Causes:**
- Technology improvement
- Cost of production ↓
- Government subsidy
- Taxes ↓

#### Decrease in Supply
$$\boxed{\text{Supply Curve shifts LEFT} \Rightarrow \text{At same P, lower } Q_s}$$

**Causes:**
- Outdated technology
- Cost of production ↑
- Taxes ↑
- Adverse weather (agriculture)

---

## 📊 9. EFFECTS OF CHANGES ON EQUILIBRIUM

### Effects of Demand Changes

#### Increase in Demand
$$\boxed{\text{Demand} \uparrow \Rightarrow \begin{cases} P_e \uparrow \\ Q_e \uparrow \end{cases}}$$

**Result:** Higher equilibrium price AND higher equilibrium quantity

**Example:** *"Demand for smartphones increases"*
- Price ↑
- Quantity sold ↑

---

#### Decrease in Demand
$$\boxed{\text{Demand} \downarrow \Rightarrow \begin{cases} P_e \downarrow \\ Q_e \downarrow \end{cases}}$$

**Result:** Lower equilibrium price AND lower equilibrium quantity

**Example:** *"Demand for DVDs decreases (streaming alternatives)"*
- Price ↓
- Quantity sold ↓

---

### Effects of Supply Changes

#### Increase in Supply
$$\boxed{\text{Supply} \uparrow \Rightarrow \begin{cases} P_e \downarrow \\ Q_e \uparrow \end{cases}}$$

**Result:** Lower equilibrium price BUT higher equilibrium quantity

**Example:** *"Good harvest increases wheat supply"*
- Price ↓
- Quantity available ↑

---

#### Decrease in Supply
$$\boxed{\text{Supply} \downarrow \Rightarrow \begin{cases} P_e \uparrow \\ Q_e \downarrow \end{cases}}$$

**Result:** Higher equilibrium price BUT lower equilibrium quantity

**Example:** *"Drought reduces crop supply"*
- Price ↑
- Quantity available ↓

## 📋 10. SUMMARY TABLE: Movement vs. Shift

| Aspect | Movement | Shift |
|--------|----------|-------|
| **Cause** | Change in **own price** | Change in **other factors** |
| **Demand Movement** | Extension (P↓) or Contraction (P↑) | Increase (right) or Decrease (left) |
| **Supply Movement** | Expansion (P↑) or Contraction (P↓) | Increase (right) or Decrease (left) |
| **Curve** | **Same curve**, different point | **New curve** |
| **Example (Demand)** | "Price falls, buy more" | "Income rises, buy more" |
| **Example (Supply)** | "Price rises, produce more" | "Technology improves, produce more" |

## 🎯 11. TYPES OF GOODS

### By Demand Response to Income

#### Normal Goods
$$\boxed{\text{Income} \uparrow \Rightarrow \text{Demand} \uparrow}$$

**Examples:** Cars, electronics, dining out

---

#### Inferior Goods
$$\boxed{\text{Income} \uparrow \Rightarrow \text{Demand} \downarrow}$$

**Examples:** Low-quality products, public transportation (for high earners)

---

### By Demand Response to Related Goods

#### Substitutes
$$\boxed{P_B \uparrow \Rightarrow Q_{d,A} \uparrow}$$

**Where:**
- PB = Price of good B
- Qd,A = Quantity demanded of good A

**Examples:** Tea & Coffee, Butter & Margarine

---

#### Complements
$$\boxed{P_B \uparrow \Rightarrow Q_{d,A} \downarrow}$$

**Examples:** Cars & Gasoline, Printers & Ink
