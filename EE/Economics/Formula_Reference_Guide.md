# CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION & EFFICIENCY

---

## 1. Technical Efficiency

### Formula:
$$\boxed{\text{Technical Efficiency} = \frac{\text{Output}}{\text{Input}}}$$

### Variables:
- **Output** = Physical output produced (kW, units, hp)
- **Input** = Physical input consumed (fuel, raw material)

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ Physical system analysis | Analyzing engines, machines, furnaces |
| ✓ Equipment performance | Comparing different equipment efficiency |
| ✓ Energy conversion | Power plants, motors, generators |

### Example Statement:
*"A diesel engine consumes 5 liters/hour and produces 100 kW. If 1 liter fuel = 25 kW potential, find technical efficiency."*

### Priority: ⭐ (Definition, less calculation-heavy)

---

## 2. Economic Efficiency (Productivity)

### Formula:
$$\boxed{\text{Economic Efficiency} = \frac{\text{Annual Revenue}}{\text{Annual Expenses}}}$$

### Variables:
- **Revenue** = Total annual income (Rs., $)
- **Expenses** = Total annual costs (Rs., $)

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ Business performance | Overall company profitability |
| ✓ Comparing alternatives | Which investment is more economically viable |
| ✓ Productivity measurement | How efficiently resources are used |

### Example Statement:
*"A business generates Rs. 50 lakhs revenue with Rs. 40 lakhs expenses. Calculate economic efficiency."*

### Priority: ⭐⭐ (Important concept)

---

## 3. Marginal Cost (MC) 🔴

### Formula:
$$\boxed{MC = \frac{\Delta TC}{\Delta Q} = \frac{TC_2 - TC_1}{Q_2 - Q_1}}$$

### Variables:
- **MC** = Marginal Cost (Rs./unit, $/unit) - cost of ONE MORE unit
- **ΔTC** = Change in Total Cost (TC₂ - TC₁)
- **ΔQ** = Change in Quantity (Q₂ - Q₁)
- **TC** = Total Cost (Rs., $)
- **Q** = Quantity (units)

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ "Find cost of next unit" | Production decision making |
| ✓ "Should we produce more?" | Compare MC with MR for profit maximization |
| ✓ Given TC at two quantities | Calculate per-unit cost change |

### Example Statement:
*"Total cost is Rs. 1000 at 10 units and Rs. 1200 at 12 units. Find marginal cost."*

### Priority: ⭐⭐ (Core concept, frequently appears)

---

## 4. Marginal Revenue (MR) 🔴

### Formula:
$$\boxed{MR = \frac{\Delta TR}{\Delta Q} = \frac{TR_2 - TR_1}{Q_2 - Q_1}}$$

### Variables:
- **MR** = Marginal Revenue (Rs./unit) - revenue from ONE MORE unit
- **ΔTR** = Change in Total Revenue (TR₂ - TR₁)
- **ΔQ** = Change in Quantity sold
- **TR** = Total Revenue (Rs., $)

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ "Revenue from next sale" | Pricing decisions |
| ✓ "Maximize profit" | Set production where MR = MC |
| ✓ Given TR at two sales levels | Calculate per-unit revenue |

### Example Statement:
*"Revenue is Rs. 5000 at 100 units and Rs. 5400 at 108 units. Find marginal revenue."*

### Priority: ⭐⭐ (Paired with MC)

---

## 5. Sunk Cost (Concept)

### Rule:
$$\boxed{\text{Sunk Cost} \Rightarrow \text{IGNORE in future analysis}}$$

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Rule |
|-----------|---------------|
| ✓ Replacement analysis | Don't consider original purchase price |
| ✓ Equipment upgrade | Use current market value only |
| ✓ Past investments | Focus on incremental future costs/benefits |

### Example Statement:
*"Equipment bought 3 years ago for Rs. 1,00,000 now worth Rs. 60,000. Which value for replacement analysis?"*  
**Answer:** Rs. 60,000 (ignore sunk cost of Rs. 1,00,000)

### Priority: ⭐ (Concept, not calculation)

---

## 6. Opportunity Cost (Concept)

### Formula:
$$\boxed{\text{Opportunity Cost of A} = \text{Benefit of Best Alternative B}}$$

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Concept |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ Choosing between alternatives | What are you giving up? |
| ✓ Resource allocation | Limited budget decisions |
| ✓ "Should we invest in X or Y?" | Compare foregone benefits |

### Example Statement:
*"Rs. 10 billion for Health or Education. Choosing Health, what's opportunity cost?"*  
**Answer:** Educational benefits foregone

### Priority: ⭐ (Concept)

# CHAPTER 2: COST & BREAKEVEN ANALYSIS 🔥

---

## 7. Total Sales Revenue (S or TR)

### Formula:
$$\boxed{S = s \times Q}$$

### Variables:
- **S (or TR)** = Total Sales/Total Revenue (Rs., $)
- **s** = Selling price per unit (Rs./unit, $/unit)
- **Q** = Quantity sold (units)

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ "Find total sales" | Given price and quantity |
| ✓ Revenue calculation | Any sales income problem |
| ✓ Building breakeven table | Calculate sales column |

### Example Statement:
*"Selling price = $50/unit, 100 units sold. Find total sales."*

### Priority: ⭐⭐ (Basic, used everywhere)

---

## 8. Total Cost (TC)

### Formula:
$$\boxed{TC = TVC + TFC = (v \times Q) + FC}$$

### Variables:
- **TC** = Total Cost (Rs., $)
- **TVC** = Total Variable Cost (Rs., $)
- **TFC (or FC)** = Total Fixed Cost / Fixed Cost (Rs., $)
- **v** = Variable cost per unit (Rs./unit)
- **Q** = Quantity produced (units)

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ "Find total production cost" | Given FC, v, and Q |
| ✓ Cost at any output level | Breakeven analysis |
| ✓ Building cost table | Calculate TC column |

### Example Statement:
*"Fixed cost = $500, variable cost = $30/unit, Q = 25. Find total cost."*

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐ (Essential)

---

## 9. Profit 💰

### Formula:
$$\boxed{\text{Profit} = S - TC = Q(s - v) - FC}$$

### Variables:
- **Profit** = Net Profit (positive) or Loss (negative)
- **S** = Total Sales
- **TC** = Total Cost
- **Q** = Quantity
- **s** = Selling price per unit
- **v** = Variable cost per unit
- **FC** = Fixed Cost

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ "Find profit at Q units" | Any profitability question |
| ✓ "Is there profit or loss?" | Compare at different output levels |
| ✓ Verify breakeven point | Profit should = 0 at BEQ |

### Example Statement:
*"s = $50, v = $30, FC = $500, Q = 30. Find profit."*

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐ (Most common question)

---

## 10. Breakeven Quantity (BEQ) 🔴🔴🔴

### Formula:
$$\boxed{BEQ = \frac{FC}{s - v}}$$

### Variables:
- **BEQ** = Breakeven Quantity (units) - minimum units to cover all costs
- **FC** = Fixed Cost (Rs., $)
- **s** = Selling price per unit
- **v** = Variable cost per unit
- **(s - v)** = Contribution per unit

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ "Find breakeven point" | **#1 MOST COMMON QUESTION** |
| ✓ "Minimum units needed" | Determine production targets |
| ✓ "When do we start making profit?" | Units where profit = 0 |
| ✓ Given FC, s, v | Almost every cost problem |

### Example Statement:
*"FC = $500, s = $50/unit, v = $30/unit. Find breakeven quantity."*

### Answer Template:
```
BEQ = 500 / (50 - 30) = 500 / 20 = 25 units
```

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐ **HIGHEST PRIORITY**

---

## 11. Breakeven Sales

### Formula:
$$\boxed{\text{Breakeven Sales} = BEQ \times s = \frac{FC \times s}{s - v}}$$

### Variables:
- **Breakeven Sales** = Sales revenue at breakeven (Rs., $)
- **BEQ** = Breakeven Quantity
- **s** = Selling price per unit

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ "Find breakeven sales revenue" | Want answer in Rs./$ not units |
| ✓ "Minimum sales needed" | Revenue-based breakeven |

### Example Statement:
*"BEQ = 25 units, s = $50. Find breakeven sales."*

### Priority: ⭐⭐ (Alternative to BEQ)

---

## 12. Contribution

### Formula:
$$\boxed{\text{Contribution per unit} = s - v}$$
$$\boxed{\text{Total Contribution} = Q(s - v)}$$

### Variables:
- **Contribution** = Amount per unit for FC and profit
- **s** = Selling price per unit
- **v** = Variable cost per unit
- **Q** = Quantity

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ Building block for BEQ | BEQ = FC / Contribution |
| ✓ "How much per unit for fixed costs?" | Profitability per unit |
| ✓ Calculating P/V ratio | P/V = Contribution/Sales |

### Example Statement:
*"s = $50, v = $30. Find contribution per unit."*

### Priority: ⭐⭐ (Component of other formulas)

---

## 13. Margin of Safety

### Formula:
$$\boxed{\text{Margin of Safety} = \text{Actual Sales} - \text{BE Sales}}$$
$$\boxed{\text{MOS \%} = \frac{\text{Actual Sales} - \text{BE Sales}}{\text{Actual Sales}} \times 100}$$

### Variables:
- **MOS** = Margin of Safety - safety buffer above breakeven
- **Actual Sales** = Current sales revenue
- **BE Sales** = Breakeven sales

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ "How much can sales drop?" | Risk assessment |
| ✓ "Safety cushion above breakeven" | Investment safety analysis |
| ✓ Given actual and BE sales | Compare alternatives |

### Example Statement:
*"Actual sales = $2500, BE sales = $1250. Find margin of safety and %."*

### Priority: ⭐⭐ (Risk analysis)

---

## 14. P/V Ratio (Profit-Volume Ratio) 🔴🔴

### Formula:
$$\boxed{\text{P/V Ratio} = \frac{s - v}{s} \times 100 = \frac{\text{Contribution}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100}$$

### Variables:
- **P/V Ratio** = Profit-Volume Ratio (%) - contribution per rupee of sales
- **s** = Selling price per unit
- **v** = Variable cost per unit

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ "Contribution per dollar of sales" | Profitability metric |
| ✓ Alternative BEQ calculation | BEQ = FC / (P/V Ratio × s) |
| ✓ "Find profit at sales level X" | Profit = (Sales × P/V) - FC |

### Example Statement:
*"s = $50, v = $30. Find P/V ratio."*

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐ (Very important, multiple uses)

---

### Related P/V Formulas:

#### Profit using P/V Ratio:
$$\boxed{\text{Profit} = (\text{Sales} \times \text{P/V Ratio}) - FC}$$

**When to use:** Calculate profit from sales amount (not quantity)

---

#### Required Sales for Target Profit:
$$\boxed{\text{Required Sales} = \frac{FC + \text{Desired Profit}}{\text{P/V Ratio}}}$$

**When to use:** *"What sales needed for Rs. X profit?"*

**Example:** *"FC = $500, P/V = 40%, desired profit = $300. Find required sales."*

## 15. Equilibrium Condition ⚖️

### Formula:
$$\boxed{Q_d = Q_s}$$

**At equilibrium:**
$$\boxed{P_e, Q_e \text{ where demand = supply}}$$

### Variables:
- **Qd** = Quantity Demanded
- **Qs** = Quantity Supplied
- **Pe** = Equilibrium Price
- **Qe** = Equilibrium Quantity

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ "Find equilibrium price/quantity" | Market clearing conditions |
| ✓ Given demand and supply equations | Set Qd = Qs, solve for P |
| ✓ "Find market equilibrium" | Where no shortage/surplus |

### Example Statement:
*"Demand: Qd = 100 - 2P. Supply: Qs = 20 + 3P. Find equilibrium."*

**Solution Template:**
```
100 - 2P = 20 + 3P
80 = 5P
Pe = 16
Qe = 100 - 2(16) = 68
```

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐ (Core market analysis)

---

## 16. Market Disequilibria

### Excess Demand (Shortage):
$$\boxed{Q_d > Q_s \Rightarrow \text{Price} \uparrow}$$

### Excess Supply (Surplus):
$$\boxed{Q_s > Q_d \Rightarrow \text{Price} \downarrow}$$

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Concept |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ "Will price rise or fall?" | Predict price movement |
| ✓ Market not in equilibrium | Shortage/surplus situations |
| ✓ "Qd = 100, Qs = 60 at P = $10" | Calculate excess demand = 40 |

### Priority: ⭐⭐ (Conceptual)

# CHAPTER 4: ELASTICITY 🔥🔥🔥

---

## 17. Price Elasticity of Demand (PED) - Basic 🔴🔴🔴

### Formula:
$$\boxed{PED = \left| \frac{\% \Delta Q_d}{\% \Delta P} \right| = \left| \frac{\Delta Q_d / Q_d}{\Delta P / P} \right|}$$

### Variables:
- **PED** = Price Elasticity of Demand (unitless, always positive)
- **ΔQd** = Change in Quantity Demanded (Qd₂ - Qd₁)
- **Qd** = Original Quantity Demanded
- **ΔP** = Change in Price (P₂ - P₁)
- **P** = Original Price

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ "Find price elasticity" | Basic elasticity question |
| ✓ Given % changes | "P rises 10%, Qd falls 15%" |
| ✓ Simple calculations | Quick estimates |

### Example Statement:
*"Price rises by 10%, quantity demanded falls by 15%. Calculate PED."*

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐ **HIGHEST**

---

## 18. PED - Midpoint Method 🔴🔴🔴

### Formula:
$$\boxed{PED = \frac{|Q_2 - Q_1|}{|P_2 - P_1|} \times \frac{(P_2 + P_1)/2}{(Q_2 + Q_1)/2}}$$

### Variables:
- **Q₁, Q₂** = Quantity at two points
- **P₁, P₂** = Price at two points
- **(Q₂+Q₁)/2** = Midpoint of quantity (average)
- **(P₂+P₁)/2** = Midpoint of price (average)

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ **"Given two (P,Q) points"** | **#1 MOST COMMON ELASTICITY QUESTION** |
| ✓ "Calculate elasticity between A and B" | Direction-independent answer |
| ✓ Exact values given | More accurate than basic method |

### Example Statement:
*"At P = $200, Q = 12. At P = $250, Q = 8. Find PED."*

### Solution Template:
```
Pmid = (200+250)/2 = 225
Qmid = (12+8)/2 = 10
%ΔP = |250-200|/225 = 50/225 = 22.2%
%ΔQ = |8-12|/10 = 4/10 = 40%
PED = 40/22.2 = 1.8
```

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐ **HIGHEST PRIORITY**

---

## 19. Total Revenue (TR)

### Formula:
$$\boxed{TR = P \times Q}$$

### Variables:
- **TR** = Total Revenue (Rs., $)
- **P** = Price per unit
- **Q** = Quantity sold

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ Analyzing revenue changes | Compare TR before/after price change |
| ✓ With elasticity | Determine if price change increases revenue |

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐ (Used with PED)

---

## 20. TR-Elasticity Relationships 🔴🔴

### Formula:
$$\boxed{\begin{cases}
\text{PED < 1 (Inelastic):} & P \uparrow \Rightarrow TR \uparrow \\
\text{PED > 1 (Elastic):} & P \uparrow \Rightarrow TR \downarrow \\
\text{PED = 1:} & P \text{ change} \Rightarrow TR \text{ unchanged}
\end{cases}}$$

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Rule |
|-----------|---------------|
| ✓ **"Should we raise price?"** | **CRITICAL BUSINESS DECISION** |
| ✓ "Will price increase help revenue?" | Check elasticity first |
| ✓ Pricing strategy | Inelastic → raise price, Elastic → lower price |

### Example Statement:
*"Demand is inelastic (PED = 0.8). Price increases from $1 to $3. Quantity falls 100→80. Verify TR increases."*

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐ **HIGHEST** (Strategy)

---

## 21. Income Elasticity (YED) 🔴🔴

### Formula:
$$\boxed{YED = \frac{\% \Delta Q_d}{\% \Delta Y} = \frac{\Delta Q_d / Q_d}{\Delta Y / Y}}$$

### Variables:
- **YED** = Income Elasticity of Demand
- **ΔQd** = Change in Quantity Demanded
- **Qd** = Original Quantity
- **ΔY** = Change in Income
- **Y** = Original Income

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ "Effect of income change on demand" | Income changes, find demand impact |
| ✓ "Classify good as luxury/necessity" | YED > 1 = luxury, 0-1 = necessity |
| ✓ "Is this inferior good?" | YED < 0 = inferior |

### Example Statement:
*"Income rises from Rs. 500 to Rs. 600. Demand increases from 20 to 25 units. Find YED and classify good."*

### Solution Template:
```
ΔY = 100, Y = 500
ΔQd = 5, Qd = 20
YED = (5/20) / (100/500) = 0.25 / 0.20 = 1.25
YED > 1 → Luxury good
```

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐ (Common)

---

## 22. Cross-Price Elasticity (XED) 🔴

### Formula:
$$\boxed{XED = \frac{\% \Delta Q_{d,A}}{\% \Delta P_B} = \frac{\Delta Q_{d,A} / Q_{d,A}}{\Delta P_B / P_B}}$$

### Variables:
- **XED** = Cross-Price Elasticity (keep sign!)
- **ΔQd,A** = Change in demand for good A
- **Qd,A** = Original demand for good A
- **ΔPB** = Change in price of good B
- **PB** = Original price of good B

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ "Are A and B substitutes or complements?" | **RELATIONSHIP QUESTION** |
| ✓ Two related goods | Price of one affects demand of other |
| ✓ Competitive analysis | Market relationships |

### Interpretation:
- **XED > 0:** Substitutes (Tea & Coffee)
- **XED < 0:** Complements (Cars & Gas)
- **XED = 0:** Unrelated

### Example Statement:
*"Rice price rises Rs. 2000→2500. Wheat demand rises 40→50 kg. Find XED and relationship."*

### Priority: ⭐⭐⭐ (Frequent)

---

## 23. Price Elasticity of Supply (PES) 🔴

### Formula:
$$\boxed{PES = \frac{\% \Delta Q_s}{\% \Delta P} = \frac{\Delta Q_s / Q_s}{\Delta P / P}}$$

### Variables:
- **PES** = Price Elasticity of Supply
- **ΔQs** = Change in Quantity Supplied
- **Qs** = Original Quantity Supplied
- **ΔP** = Change in Price
- **P** = Original Price

### When to Use:
| Condition | Use This Formula |
|-----------|------------------|
| ✓ "How responsive is supply to price?" | Supply flexibility |
| ✓ Short-run vs long-run comparison | Time period analysis |
| ✓ Given price and supply data | Calculate supply elasticity |

### Example Statement:
*"Price rises from Rs. 10 to Rs. 12. Supply increases from 20 to 24 units. Find PES."*

### Priority: ⭐⭐ (Less common than demand elasticity)

# 📊 PRIORITY SUMMARY

---

## ⭐⭐⭐ HIGHEST PRIORITY (Master These First)

1. **BEQ = FC/(s-v)** - Appears in 90% of cost problems

3. **Profit = Q(s-v) - FC** - Every profitability question
4. **TR = P × Q** with **TR-Elasticity Rules** - Pricing strategy

7. **XED** - Substitutes/complements
8. **P/V Ratio = (s-v)/s × 100** - Alternative calculations

13. **PES** - Supply responsiveness
14. **Economic Efficiency** - Productivity
