# CHAPTER 1: Introduction to Economics

## CONCEPT REVIEW

### 4 Factors of Production
1. **LAND** - Natural Resources (water, oil, gas, trees)
2. **LABOR** - Physical and intellectual manpower
3. **CAPITAL** - Tools, machinery, factories
4. **ENTREPRENEURSHIP** - Investment money and risk-taking

### Production Flow
```
Land + Labor + Capital + Entrepreneurship
    ↓
Production/Manufacturing
    ↓
Goods & Services
    ↓
Consumers
```

# CHAPTER 3: Interest Formulas & Applications

## KEY FORMULAS

1. **Single-Payment Compound Amount:** F = P(1 + i)^n
2. **Single-Payment Present Worth:** P = F/(1 + i)^n
3. **Equal-Payment Series Compound Amount:** F = A × [((1+i)^n - 1) / i]
4. **Equal-Payment Series Sinking Fund:** A = F × [i / ((1+i)^n - 1)]
5. **Equal-Payment Series Present Worth:** P = A × [((1+i)^n - 1) / (i(1+i)^n)]

---

## PRACTICE PROBLEMS

### Problem 3.1: Single-Payment Compound Amount (From Slide Example)

**Question:**
A person deposits a sum of Rs. 20,000 at the interest rate of 18% compounded annually for 10 years. Find the maturity value after 10 years.

**Solution:**

**Given:**
- P = Rs. 20,000
- i = 18% = 0.18
- n = 10 years

**Formula:**
$$F = P(1 + i)^n$$

**Calculation:**
$$F = 20,000 \times (1.18)^{10}$$

Step 1: Calculate (1.18)^10
$$(1.18)^{10} = 5.2338$$

Step 2: Calculate F
$$F = 20,000 \times 5.2338 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 1,04,676}$$

**Answer:** The maturity value after 10 years will be **Rs. 1,04,676**

---

### Problem 3.2: Single-Payment Present Worth (From Slide Example)

**Question:**
A person wishes to have a future sum of Rs. 1,00,000 for his son's education after 10 years from now. What is the single-payment that he should deposit now so that he gets the desired amount after 10 years? The bank gives 15% interest rate compounded annually.

**Solution:**

**Given:**
- F = Rs. 1,00,000
- i = 15% = 0.15
- n = 10 years
- Find: P = ?

**Formula:**
$$P = \frac{F}{(1 + i)^n}$$

**Calculation:**
$$P = \frac{1,00,000}{(1.15)^{10}}$$

Step 1: Calculate (1.15)^10
$$(1.15)^{10} = 4.0456$$

Step 2: Calculate P
$$P = \frac{1,00,000}{4.0456} = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 24,718}$$

**Answer:** He should deposit **Rs. 24,718 today** to get Rs. 1,00,000 after 10 years.

**Verification:**
$$24,718 \times (1.15)^{10} = 24,718 \times 4.0456 \approx 1,00,000$$ ✓

### Problem 3.3: Equal-Payment Series Compound Amount (From Slide Example)

**Question:**
A person who is now 35 years old is planning for his retired life. He plans to invest an equal sum of Rs. 10,000 at the end of every year for the next 25 years starting from the end of the next year. The bank gives 20% interest rate, compounded annually. Find the maturity value of his account when he is 60 years old.

**Solution:**

**Given:**
- A = Rs. 10,000 per year
- i = 20% = 0.20
- n = 25 years

**Formula:**
$$F = A \times \frac{(1+i)^n - 1}{i}$$

**Calculation:**
$$F = 10,000 \times \frac{(1.20)^{25} - 1}{0.20}$$

Step 1: Calculate (1.20)^25
$$(1.20)^{25} = 95.396$$

Step 2: Calculate the factor
$$\frac{95.396 - 1}{0.20} = \frac{94.396}{0.20} = 471.98$$

Step 3: Calculate F
$$F = 10,000 \times 471.98 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 47,19,800}$$

**Answer:** At age 60, he will have **Rs. 47,19,800** (approximately Rs. 47.2 lakhs)

**Interpretation:** By investing just Rs. 10,000 per year, the power of compound interest over 25 years at 20% results in nearly Rs. 47.2 lakhs!

### Problem 3.4: Equal-Payment Series Sinking Fund

**Question:**
A company has to replace a present facility after 15 years at an outlay of Rs. 5,00,000. It plans to deposit an equal amount at the end of every year for the next 15 years at an interest rate of 12% compounded annually. What should be the annual deposit?

**Solution:**

**Given:**
- F = Rs. 5,00,000 (future sum needed)
- i = 12% = 0.12
- n = 15 years
- Find: A = ?

**Formula:**
$$A = F \times \frac{i}{(1+i)^n - 1}$$

**Calculation:**
$$A = 5,00,000 \times \frac{0.12}{(1.12)^{15} - 1}$$

Step 1: Calculate (1.12)^15
$$(1.12)^{15} = 5.4736$$

Step 2: Calculate the factor
$$\frac{0.12}{5.4736 - 1} = \frac{0.12}{4.4736} = 0.02682$$

Step 3: Calculate A
$$A = 5,00,000 \times 0.02682 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 13,410}$$

**Answer:** The company should deposit **Rs. 13,410 annually** for 15 years.

**Verification:** Rs. 13,410 × 37.28 (series factor) ≈ Rs. 5,00,000 ✓

### Problem 3.5: Present Worth of Annuity

**Question:**
What is the present value of receiving Rs. 15,000 at the end of each year for the next 8 years if the interest rate is 10% compounded annually?

**Solution:**

**Given:**
- A = Rs. 15,000 per year
- i = 10% = 0.10
- n = 8 years

**Formula:**
$$P = A \times \frac{(1+i)^n - 1}{i(1+i)^n}$$

**Calculation:**
$$P = 15,000 \times \frac{(1.10)^8 - 1}{0.10 \times (1.10)^8}$$

Step 1: Calculate (1.10)^8
$$(1.10)^8 = 2.1436$$

Step 2: Calculate numerator
$$2.1436 - 1 = 1.1436$$

Step 3: Calculate denominator
$$0.10 \times 2.1436 = 0.21436$$

Step 4: Calculate factor
$$\frac{1.1436}{0.21436} = 5.3349$$

Step 5: Calculate P
$$P = 15,000 \times 5.3349 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 80,024}$$

**Answer:** The present value is **Rs. 80,024**

# CHAPTER 4: Present Worth Method

## METHOD OVERVIEW

**Purpose:** Compare investment alternatives by reducing all cash flows to present value (time zero)

**Decision Rules:**
- **Revenue-dominated:** Select alternative with **highest (most positive) PW**
- **Cost-dominated:** Select alternative with **lowest cost (least negative) PW**

---

## PRACTICE PROBLEMS

### Problem 4.1: Technology Selection (From Slide Example)

**Question:**
Alpha Industry is planning to expand its production operation. It has identified three different technologies for meeting the goal. The initial outlay and annual revenues are:

| Technology | Initial Investment | Annual Revenue | Life (years) |
|------------|-------------------|----------------|--------------|
| A | Rs. 10,00,000 | Rs. 3,50,000 | 5 |
| B | Rs. 15,00,000 | Rs. 4,80,000 | 5 |
| C | Rs. 18,00,000 | Rs. 5,50,000 | 5 |

Suggest the best technology at 20% interest rate, compounded annually.

**Solution:**

**PV Annuity Factor at 20% for 5 years:**
$$\text{PV Factor} = \frac{(1.20)^5 - 1}{0.20 \times (1.20)^5} = \frac{2.4883 - 1}{0.20 \times 2.4883} = \frac{1.4883}{0.4977} = 2.9906$$

**Technology A:**
$$PW_A = -10,00,000 + (3,50,000 \times 2.9906)$$
$$PW_A = -10,00,000 + 10,46,710 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } +46,710}$$

**Technology B:**
$$PW_B = -15,00,000 + (4,80,000 \times 2.9906)$$
$$PW_B = -15,00,000 + 14,35,488 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } -64,512}$$

**Technology C:**
$$PW_C = -18,00,000 + (5,50,000 \times 2.9906)$$
$$PW_C = -18,00,000 + 16,44,830 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } -1,55,170}$$

**Comparison:**
| Technology | PW | Rank |
|------------|-----|------|
| A | +46,710 | **1st (Best)** |
| B | -64,512 | 2nd |
| C | -1,55,170 | 3rd |

**Decision: Technology A** - It has the highest (only positive) present worth, providing Rs. 46,710 in value.

### Problem 4.2: Elevator Bid Comparison (From Slide Example)

**Question:**
An engineer has two bids for an elevator to be installed in a new building:

| Factor | Bid A | Bid B |
|--------|-------|-------|
| Initial Cost | Rs. 8,00,000 | Rs. 10,00,000 |
| Annual Maintenance | Rs. 50,000 | Rs. 30,000 |
| Salvage Value | Rs. 1,00,000 | Rs. 1,50,000 |
| Life | 10 years | 10 years |

Determine which bid should be accepted at 15% interest rate, compounded annually.

**Solution:**

**PV Annuity Factor at 15% for 10 years:**
$$\text{PV Factor} = \frac{(1.15)^{10} - 1}{0.15 \times (1.15)^{10}} = \frac{4.0456 - 1}{0.15 \times 4.0456} = \frac{3.0456}{0.6068} = 5.0188$$

**PV Factor for Salvage:**
$$\text{PV of Salvage} = \frac{1}{(1.15)^{10}} = \frac{1}{4.0456} = 0.2472$$

**Bid A:**
$$PW_A = -8,00,000 - (50,000 \times 5.0188) + (1,00,000 \times 0.2472)$$
$$PW_A = -8,00,000 - 2,50,940 + 24,720$$
$$PW_A = \boxed{\text{Rs. } -10,26,220}$$

**Bid B:**
$$PW_B = -10,00,000 - (30,000 \times 5.0188) + (1,50,000 \times 0.2472)$$
$$PW_B = -10,00,000 - 1,50,564 + 37,080$$
$$PW_B = \boxed{\text{Rs. } -11,13,484}$$

**Comparison:**
| Bid | Present Worth | Rank |
|-----|---------------|------|
| A | -10,26,220 | **1st (Better)** |
| B | -11,13,484 | 2nd |

**Decision: Bid A** - It has the lower cost (less negative PW) with savings of Rs. 87,264

### Problem 4.3: Down Payment vs Installment

**Question:**
A granite company is planning to buy a fully automated granite cutting machine. 
- **Down Payment Option:** Rs. 16,00,000
- **Installment Option:** 25% down payment + Rs. 2,00,000 annually for 10 years

$$PW_1 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } -16,00,000}$$

**Option 2: Installment**

Initial payment = 25% of 16,00,000 = Rs. 4,00,000

$$PW_2 = -4,00,000 - 8,98,820$$

**Comparison:**
| Option | Present Worth | Decision |
|--------|---------------|----------|
| Down Payment | -16,00,000 | — |

# CHAPTER 5: Elasticity & Its Application

## KEY CONCEPTS

**Price Elasticity of Demand (PED):** Measures responsiveness of quantity demanded to price changes

**Midpoint Formula:**
$$PED = \frac{\Delta Q / \text{Average Q}}{\Delta P / \text{Average P}}$$

---

## PRACTICE PROBLEMS

### Problem 5.1: Hotel Rooms (From Slide Example)

**Question:**
Calculate the price elasticity of demand for hotel rooms using the midpoint method:
- If P = $70, Q_d = 5,000
- If P = $90, Q_d = 3,000

**Solution:**

**Given:**
- (P₁, Q₁) = ($70, 5,000)
- (P₂, Q₂) = ($90, 3,000)

**Step 1: Calculate changes**
- ΔP = 90 - 70 = 20
- ΔQ = 3,000 - 5,000 = -2,000

**Step 2: Calculate averages**
- Average P = (70 + 90) / 2 = 80
- Average Q = (5,000 + 3,000) / 2 = 4,000

**Step 3: Calculate percentage changes**
- % Change in P = 20 / 80 = 0.25 (25%)
- % Change in Q = -2,000 / 4,000 = -0.50 (-50%)

**Step 4: Calculate PED**
$$PED = \frac{-0.50}{0.25} = \boxed{-2.0}$$

**Interpretation:**
- |PED| = 2.0 > 1 → **ELASTIC demand**
- A 1% increase in price leads to 2% decrease in quantity demanded
- Hotel rooms are price-sensitive; customers respond strongly to price changes

---

### Problem 5.2: Insulin vs Caribbean Cruises

**Question:**
The prices of both insulin and Caribbean cruises rise by 20%. 
a) For which good does Q_d drop the most? Why?
b) What does this tell us about price elasticity?

**Solution:**

**a) Which drops most:**

**Insulin:**
- Essential medicine for diabetics - **NECESSITY**
- Few or no substitutes available
- Cannot be postponed or eliminated
- Q_d will drop **very little** (maybe 2-5%)
- **Inelastic demand**

**Caribbean Cruises:**
- Vacation/leisure activity - **LUXURY**
- Many substitutes (other destinations, stay home)
- Can be easily postponed
- Q_d will drop **significantly** (maybe 30-50%)
- **Elastic demand**

**Answer:** Caribbean cruise demand drops the most.

**b) Price Elasticity Insight:**

| Good Type | PED | Interpretation |
|-----------|-----|----------------|
| Insulin | |PED| < 1 | Inelastic - necessity |
| Cruises | |PED| > 1 | Elastic - luxury |

**Key Lesson:** Price elasticity is **higher for luxuries** than for **necessities**

Rank the following from most elastic to least elastic demand:

### Problem 5.4: Time Horizon Effect

**Question:**
Gasoline prices increase by 40%.
a) What happens to quantity demanded in 1 week?
b) What happens to quantity demanded in 3 years?
c) Explain why the responses differ.

**Solution:**

**a) Short Run (1 week):**
- Q_d drops by approximately **5-10%**
- |PED| ≈ 0.15 (Very inelastic)
- People still need to drive to work, school
- Can't change habits immediately

**b) Long Run (3 years):**
- Q_d drops by approximately **30-40%**
- |PED| ≈ 0.85 (More elastic)
- People buy fuel-efficient cars
- Move closer to work
- Use public transportation
- Carpool arrangements

**c) Why Different:**

| Time Period | Elasticity | Reason |
|-------------|-----------|--------|
| Short Run | **Less elastic** | Habits fixed, contracts committed, no time to adjust |
| Long Run | **More elastic** | Can change behavior, make new arrangements, find substitutes |

**Key Principle:** Demand is **more elastic over longer time horizons** because people have more time to adjust their behavior.

# ELEMENT OF COST & BREAK-EVEN ANALYSIS

## KEY FORMULAS

1. **Total Cost:** TC = FC + VC = FC + (v × Q)
2. **Total Revenue:** TR = s × Q
3. **Profit:** Profit = TR - TC
4. **Break-Even Quantity:** Q_BEP = FC / (s - v)
5. **Break-Even Sales:** BEP Sales = Q_BEP × s
6. **Contribution per unit:** s - v
7. **Contribution Margin Ratio:** (s - v) / s

---

## PRACTICE PROBLEMS

### Problem BEP.1: Basic Break-Even Calculation

**Question:**
A company has the following cost structure:
- Fixed Cost = Rs. 60,000
- Variable Cost per unit = Rs. 30
- Selling Price per unit = Rs. 50

Calculate:
a) Break-even point in units
b) Break-even point in sales revenue
c) Profit if 5,000 units are sold
d) Margin of Safety at 5,000 units

**Solution:**

**a) BEP in Units:**
$$Q_{BEP} = \frac{FC}{s - v} = \frac{60,000}{50 - 30} = \frac{60,000}{20} = \boxed{3,000 \text{ units}}$$

**b) BEP in Sales Revenue:**
$$\text{BEP Sales} = Q_{BEP} \times s = 3,000 \times 50 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 1,50,000}$$

**c) Profit at 5,000 units:**
- Total Revenue = 5,000 × 50 = Rs. 2,50,000
- Total Variable Cost = 5,000 × 30 = Rs. 1,50,000
- Total Cost = 60,000 + 1,50,000 = Rs. 2,10,000
$$\text{Profit} = 2,50,000 - 2,10,000 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 40,000}$$

Or using contribution method:
- Contribution per unit = 50 - 30 = Rs. 20
- Total Contribution = 5,000 × 20 = Rs. 1,00,000
$$\text{Profit} = 1,00,000 - 60,000 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 40,000}$$ ✓

**d) Margin of Safety:**
$$\text{MOS} = \text{Actual Sales} - \text{BEP Sales}$$
$$\text{MOS} = 2,50,000 - 1,50,000 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 1,00,000}$$

Or as percentage:
$$\text{MOS\%} = \frac{1,00,000}{2,50,000} \times 100 = \boxed{40\%}$$

---

### Problem BEP.2: Target Profit

**Question:**
Using the same data as Problem BEP.1:
- FC = Rs. 60,000
- v = Rs. 30 per unit
- s = Rs. 50 per unit

Calculate:
a) How many units must be sold to earn a profit of Rs. 80,000?
b) What is the required sales revenue?

**Solution:**

**a) Units for Target Profit:**

At target profit, we need:
$$\text{Revenue} - \text{Total Cost} = \text{Target Profit}$$
$$(s \times Q) - [FC + (v \times Q)] = 80,000$$
$$50Q - [60,000 + 30Q] = 80,000$$
$$20Q = 60,000 + 80,000$$
$$20Q = 1,40,000$$
$$Q = \boxed{7,000 \text{ units}}$$

**Alternative Formula:**
$$Q = \frac{FC + \text{Target Profit}}{s - v} = \frac{60,000 + 80,000}{20} = \frac{1,40,000}{20} = 7,000$$ ✓

**b) Required Sales Revenue:**
$$\text{Sales} = 7,000 \times 50 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 3,50,000}$$

**Verification:**
- Revenue = Rs. 3,50,000
- Variable Cost = 7,000 × 30 = Rs. 2,10,000
- Fixed Cost = Rs. 60,000
- Total Cost = Rs. 2,70,000
- Profit = 3,50,000 - 2,70,000 = Rs. 80,000 ✓

---

### Problem BEP.3: Multiple Products

**Question:**
A company sells two products:

| Product | Selling Price | Variable Cost | Sales Mix |
|---------|--------------|---------------|-----------|
| X | Rs. 100 | Rs. 60 | 60% |
| Y | Rs. 150 | Rs. 90 | 40% |

Fixed Cost = Rs. 1,20,000

Calculate the overall break-even point in units.

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Calculate weighted average contribution per unit**

Product X:
- Contribution = 100 - 60 = Rs. 40
- Weighted contribution = 40 × 0.60 = Rs. 24

Product Y:
- Contribution = 150 - 90 = Rs. 60
- Weighted contribution = 60 × 0.40 = Rs. 24

**Total weighted average contribution = 24 + 24 = Rs. 48**

**Step 2: Calculate BEP**
$$Q_{BEP} = \frac{FC}{\text{Weighted Avg Contribution}} = \frac{1,20,000}{48} = \boxed{2,500 \text{ units}}$$

**Step 3: Break down by product**
- Product X: 2,500 × 60% = **1,500 units**
- Product Y: 2,500 × 40% = **1,000 units**

**Verification:**
- Contribution from X: 1,500 × 40 = Rs. 60,000
- Contribution from Y: 1,000 × 60 = Rs. 60,000
- Total Contribution = Rs. 1,20,000
- Profit = 1,20,000 - 1,20,000 = 0 ✓ (Break-even confirmed)

### Problem BEP.4: CVP Graph Interpretation

Given the following CVP graph, determine:

│           /

### Problem BEP.5: Sensitivity Analysis

**Question:**
A business has:
- FC = Rs. 50,000
- v = Rs. 25
- s = Rs. 40

Analyze the impact on BEP if:
a) Fixed costs increase by 20%
b) Variable costs increase by 20%
c) Selling price increases by 20%
d) All three changes occur simultaneously

**Solution:**

**Original BEP:**
$$Q_{BEP} = \frac{50,000}{40 - 25} = \frac{50,000}{15} = 3,333 \text{ units}$$

**a) FC increases by 20%:**
- New FC = 50,000 × 1.20 = Rs. 60,000
$$Q_{BEP} = \frac{60,000}{15} = \boxed{4,000 \text{ units}} \text{ (+20% increase)}$$

**b) VC increases by 20%:**
- New v = 25 × 1.20 = Rs. 30
$$Q_{BEP} = \frac{50,000}{40 - 30} = \frac{50,000}{10} = \boxed{5,000 \text{ units}} \text{ (+50% increase)}$$

**c) Selling price increases by 20%:**
- New s = 40 × 1.20 = Rs. 48
$$Q_{BEP} = \frac{50,000}{48 - 25} = \frac{50,000}{23} = \boxed{2,174 \text{ units}} \text{ (-35% decrease)}$$

**d) All three changes:**
- FC = Rs. 60,000
- v = Rs. 30
- s = Rs. 48
$$Q_{BEP} = \frac{60,000}{48 - 30} = \frac{60,000}{18} = \boxed{3,333 \text{ units}} \text{ (No change!)}$$

**Key Insights:**
| Change | Impact on BEP | Sensitivity |
|--------|---------------|-------------|
| FC +20% | BEP +20% | Proportional |
| VC +20% | BEP +50% | **Most sensitive** |
| Price +20% | BEP -35% | **Best improvement** |
| All together | No change | Offsetting effects |

**Conclusion:** BEP is most sensitive to variable cost changes and price is the most powerful lever for reducing BEP.

### Problem M.1: Integrated Analysis

**Question:**
A company manufactures widgets with the following details:
- Initial investment in machinery: Rs. 5,00,000
- Salvage value after 5 years: Rs. 1,00,000
- Fixed operating cost per year: Rs. 80,000
- Variable cost per unit: Rs. 40
- Selling price per unit: Rs. 70
- Interest rate: 15% compounded annually
- Expected production: 6,000 units per year

Calculate:
a) Annual profit
b) Break-even point in units
c) Present worth of the investment over 5 years
d) Should the investment be made?

**Solution:**

**a) Annual Profit:**
- Revenue = 6,000 × 70 = Rs. 4,20,000
- Variable Cost = 6,000 × 40 = Rs. 2,40,000
- Fixed Cost = Rs. 80,000
- Total Cost = 2,40,000 + 80,000 = Rs. 3,20,000
$$\text{Annual Profit} = 4,20,000 - 3,20,000 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 1,00,000}$$

**b) Break-Even Point:**
$$Q_{BEP} = \frac{80,000}{70 - 40} = \frac{80,000}{30} = \boxed{2,667 \text{ units}}$$

**c) Present Worth:**

PV annuity factor (15%, 5 years) = 3.3522
PV single payment factor (15%, 5 years) = 0.4972

$$PW = -5,00,000 + (1,00,000 \times 3.3522) + (1,00,000 \times 0.4972)$$
$$PW = -5,00,000 + 3,35,220 + 49,720$$
$$PW = \boxed{\text{Rs. } -1,15,060}$$

**d) Decision:**
**No, the investment should NOT be made** because:
- Present Worth is negative (Rs. -1,15,060)
- The project doesn't generate sufficient returns to cover the 15% required rate of return
- Although annual profit is positive, it's not enough to justify the initial investment at the given interest rate

**Alternative:** The company should either:
- Increase selling price
- Reduce costs
- Find a lower interest rate financing option
