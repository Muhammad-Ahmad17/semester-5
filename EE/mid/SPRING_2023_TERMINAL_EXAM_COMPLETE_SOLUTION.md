# QUESTION #1: Cost-Volume-Profit Analysis (CVP) [14 + 2 = 16 Marks]

## Part (a): Delta Enterprises Analysis [14 Marks]

### Given Data (Year 2024)
- **Sales (Revenue):** Rs. 300,000
- **Fixed Cost:** Rs. 60,000
- **Variable Cost:** Rs. 180,000

---

### a) CONTRIBUTION

**Definition:** Contribution is the amount remaining after deducting variable costs from sales revenue. It contributes toward covering fixed costs and generating profit.

**Formula:**
$$\text{Contribution} = \text{Sales} - \text{Variable Cost}$$

**Calculation:**
$$\text{Contribution} = 300,000 - 180,000 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 240,000}$$

**Interpretation:** The company has Rs. 240,000 available to cover fixed costs and contribute to profit.

---

### b) PROFIT

**Definition:** Profit is the net income after deducting all costs (fixed and variable) from sales revenue.

**Formula:**
$$\text{Profit} = \text{Sales} - \text{Total Cost}$$
$$\text{Profit} = \text{Contribution} - \text{Fixed Cost}$$

**Calculation:**
$$\text{Total Cost} = \text{Fixed Cost} + \text{Variable Cost} = 60,000 + 180,000 = 240,000$$

$$\text{Profit} = 300,000 - 240,000 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 60,000}$$

Or:
$$\text{Profit} = 240,000 - 60,000 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 60,000}$$

**Interpretation:** After covering all costs, the company earns a profit of Rs. 60,000 in 2024.

---

### c) BREAK-EVEN POINT (BEP)

**Definition:** Break-Even Point is the sales level at which Total Revenue equals Total Cost, resulting in zero profit/loss.

**Key Calculations:**

**Step 1: Calculate Contribution Margin Ratio (CMR)**
$$\text{CMR} = \frac{\text{Contribution}}{\text{Sales}} = \frac{240,000}{300,000} = 0.80 = 80\%$$

This means 80% of every rupee in sales contributes to covering fixed costs.

**Step 2: Calculate Variable Cost Ratio (VCR)**
$$\text{VCR} = \frac{\text{Variable Cost}}{\text{Sales}} = \frac{180,000}{300,000} = 0.20 = 20\%$$

**Step 3: BEP in Rupees (Sales)**

**Formula:**
$$\text{BEP (Sales)} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost}}{\text{CMR}}$$

$$\text{BEP (Sales)} = \frac{60,000}{0.80} = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 75,000}$$

**Step 4: Verification**

At BEP of Rs. 75,000 sales:
- Variable Cost = 75,000 × 0.20 = Rs. 15,000
- Contribution = 75,000 - 15,000 = Rs. 60,000
- Profit = 60,000 - 60,000 = Rs. 0 ✓

**Interpretation:** The company needs to achieve sales of **Rs. 75,000** to break even (no profit/loss). Below this point, there's a loss; above this point, there's profit.

---

### d) MARGIN OF SAFETY (MOS)

**Definition:** Margin of Safety represents the amount by which sales can drop before reaching the break-even point. It indicates the safety cushion or buffer.

**Formula:**
$$\text{MOS} = \text{Current Sales} - \text{BEP Sales}$$

**Calculation:**
$$\text{MOS} = 300,000 - 75,000 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 225,000}$$

**Alternative: MOS as Percentage**
$$\text{MOS \%} = \frac{\text{MOS}}{\text{Current Sales}} \times 100 = \frac{225,000}{300,000} \times 100 = \boxed{75\%}$$

**Interpretation:** 
- The company's current sales of Rs. 300,000 exceeds BEP by **Rs. 225,000**
- Sales can drop by **75%** before the company starts incurring losses
- This indicates a **strong margin of safety** - the company has good financial security

## Part (b): Demand Curve Movement vs Shift [2 Marks]

### Reason for Movement Along the Demand Curve

**Movement along the demand curve occurs when:**
- **Only the price of the product changes** (all other factors remain constant)
- The quantity demanded changes in response to this price change
- This reflects the **law of demand**: As price increases, quantity demanded decreases (and vice versa)
- The movement is **along the existing curve**, not to a new curve

**Example:** Graphics card price increases from $250 → $300, causing quantity demanded to decrease

---

### Difference Between Movement vs Shift

| Aspect | Movement Along Curve | Shift of Curve |
|--------|---|---|
| **Cause** | Price of the product changes | Non-price factors change (income, preferences, related goods, expectations, etc.) |
| **Direction** | Upward or downward along same curve | Rightward (increase) or Leftward (decrease) |
| **Demand Changes?** | NO - only quantity demanded changes | YES - entire demand changes |
| **Visual Change** | Points move on existing curve | Entire curve relocates |
| **Example** | Price ↑ → Quantity demanded ↓ | Income ↑ → Demand shifts right |

# QUESTION #2: Roofing Material Selection [4 Marks]

## Given Information

| Factor | Fiber Cement | Galvanized Steel |
|--------|---|---|
| **Cost per sheet** | Rs. 800 | Rs. 1,200 |
| **Weight per sheet** | 20 kg | 30 kg |
| **Shipping rate** | Rs. 0.80/kg/100km | Rs. 0.80/kg/100km |
| **Distance** | 180 km | 180 km |

---

## Solution

### Step 1: Calculate Material Cost (per sheet)

**Fiber Cement:**
$$\text{Material Cost}_{\text{FC}} = \text{Rs. } 800$$

**Galvanized Steel:**
$$\text{Material Cost}_{\text{GS}} = \text{Rs. } 1,200$$

---

### Step 2: Calculate Shipping Cost (per sheet)

**Shipping Cost Formula:**
$$\text{Shipping Cost} = \text{Weight (kg)} \times \text{Rate} \times \frac{\text{Distance}}{100}$$

**Fiber Cement:**
$$\text{Shipping Cost}_{\text{FC}} = 20 \times 0.80 \times \frac{180}{100}$$

$$\text{Shipping Cost}_{\text{FC}} = 20 \times 0.80 \times 1.80 = \text{Rs. } 28.80$$

**Galvanized Steel:**
$$\text{Shipping Cost}_{\text{GS}} = 30 \times 0.80 \times \frac{180}{100}$$

$$\text{Shipping Cost}_{\text{GS}} = 30 \times 0.80 \times 1.80 = \text{Rs. } 43.20$$

---

### Step 3: Calculate Total Cost per Sheet

**Fiber Cement:**
$$\text{Total Cost}_{\text{FC}} = 800 + 28.80 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 828.80}$$

**Galvanized Steel:**
$$\text{Total Cost}_{\text{GS}} = 1,200 + 43.20 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 1,243.20}$$

---

## Recommendation

$$\boxed{\text{FIBER CEMENT SHEETS SHOULD BE CHOSEN}}$$

**Economic Advantage per sheet:**
$$\text{Savings} = 1,243.20 - 828.80 = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 414.40 \text{ per sheet}}$$

**Justification:**
- Fiber Cement sheets have a **lower material cost** (Rs. 800 vs Rs. 1,200)
- Although both materials incur the same shipping rate, the **lighter weight of Fiber Cement** (20 kg vs 30 kg) results in **lower transportation costs** (Rs. 28.80 vs Rs. 43.20)
- **Total installed cost is 33.3% lower** for Fiber Cement
- Both materials meet functional and aesthetic standards, so the economic choice is clear

# QUESTION #3: Economic Definitions [1.5 × 4 = 6 Marks]

## I. Margin of Safety (MOS) vs. Break-Even Sales (BEP)

| Aspect | Margin of Safety | Break-Even Point |
|--------|---|---|
| **Definition** | The excess of current/budgeted sales above the break-even point | The sales level where Total Revenue = Total Cost |
| **Meaning** | Amount by which sales can drop before losses begin | Minimum sales required to avoid loss |
| **Formula** | MOS = Current Sales - BEP Sales | BEP = Fixed Cost ÷ CMR |
| **Indicator** | Safety cushion/buffer available | Threshold or critical point |
| **Value Relationship** | Higher is better (more safety) | Lower is better (easier to achieve) |
| **Profit at this point** | Positive (profit is made) | Zero (no profit, no loss) |
| **Example (Delta Enterprises)** | Rs. 225,000 (75% of sales) | Rs. 75,000 |
| **Interpretation** | Sales can drop 75% before losses | Need minimum Rs. 75,000 to break even |

---

## II. Microeconomics vs. Macroeconomics

| Aspect | Microeconomics | Macroeconomics |
|--------|---|---|
| **Scope** | Small scale - individual economic units | Large scale - entire economy |
| **Focus** | Individual consumers, firms, industries, markets | National income, employment, inflation, growth |
| **Decision-Makers** | Consumers and individual businesses | Government, central bank, policymakers |
| **Analysis Level** | Partial equilibrium (one market at a time) | General equilibrium (entire economy) |
| **Examples** | Why do graphics card prices change? | Why does inflation occur nationally? |
| **Key Questions** | How do firms set prices? | What causes unemployment? |
| **Time Horizon** | Short to medium term usually | Medium to long term |
| **Tools Used** | Supply & demand, elasticity, cost analysis | National income accounting, fiscal policy |

---

## III. Simple Interest Rate vs. Compound Interest Rate

| Aspect | Simple Interest | Compound Interest |
|--------|---|---|
| **Definition** | Interest calculated only on principal amount | Interest calculated on principal + accumulated interest |
| **Formula** | I = P × r × t | A = P(1+r)^n |
| **Base for Calculation** | Always on original principal | On principal + previous interest (grows) |
| **Growth Pattern** | **Linear** - grows in straight line | **Exponential** - grows at increasing rate |
| **Interest Each Year** | Same amount every year | Increases each year |
| **Final Amount** | A = P + (P × r × t) | A = P(1+r)^n |
| **Which gives more return?** | Lower returns | Higher returns (for same rate & time) |
| **Example:** Principal Rs. 1,000, Rate 10%, Time 3 years | Year 1: Rs. 100; Year 2: Rs. 100; Year 3: Rs. 100; **Total: Rs. 1,300** | Year 1: Rs. 100; Year 2: Rs. 110; Year 3: Rs. 121; **Total: Rs. 1,331** |
| **Most Common Use** | Short-term bonds, some loans | Savings accounts, investments, mortgages |
| **Graphically** | Straight line | Curve (exponential) |

---

## IV. Cross Demand vs. Price Demand

| Aspect | Cross Demand | Price Demand |
|--------|---|---|
| **Definition** | Demand for one good in response to price change of another related good | Demand for a good in response to change in its own price |
| **Variable Changed** | Price of **different/related product** changes | Price of **same product** changes |
| **Relationship** | **Substitutes:** Direct (positive) / **Complements:** Inverse (negative) | **Inverse/Negative** - as price ↑, demand ↓ |
| **Elasticity Type** | Cross Price Elasticity (XED) | Price Elasticity of Demand (PED) |
| **Examples** | - Price of Pepsi ↑ → Demand for Coke ↑ (substitutes) | - Price of graphics card ↑ → Demand ↓ |
| | - Price of petrol ↑ → Demand for cars ↓ (complements) | - Price of cars ↑ → Demand for cars ↓ |
| **Elasticity Value** | Positive (substitutes) or Negative (complements) | Always Negative |
| **Use in Economics** | Analyzing product relationships | Analyzing individual good demand |
| **Decision Making** | Competitors' pricing decisions | Own pricing strategy |

# QUESTION #4: Time Value of Money Analysis [5 + 4 = 9 Marks]

## Part (a): Machine Selection Using Present Worth Method [5 Marks]

### Given Information

| Parameter | Machine A | Machine B |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| **Initial Investment** | Rs. 1,500,000 | Rs. 2,200,000 |
| **Annual Revenue** | Rs. 500,000 | Rs. 800,000 |
| **Life** | 4 years | 4 years |
| **Interest Rate** | 18% compounded annually | 18% compounded annually |

---

### Present Worth Method

**Present Worth = -Initial Investment + PV of Annual Revenues**

**Formula for PV of Annuity:**
$$PV = \frac{A \times [(1+i)^n - 1]}{i(1+i)^n}$$

Where:
- A = Annual revenue
- i = Interest rate = 0.18
- n = Number of years = 4

---

## MACHINE A Analysis

### Step 1: Calculate PV of Annual Revenues

$$PV_{\text{Annuity}} = \frac{500,000 \times [(1.18)^4 - 1]}{0.18 \times (1.18)^4}$$

**Calculate (1.18)⁴:**
$$(1.18)^4 = 1.9388$$

$$PV_{\text{Annuity, A}} = \frac{500,000 \times (1.9388 - 1)}{0.18 \times 1.9388}$$

$$PV_{\text{Annuity, A}} = \frac{500,000 \times 0.9388}{0.3490} = \frac{469,400}{0.3490}$$

$$PV_{\text{Annuity, A}} = \text{Rs. } 1,345,559$$

**Alternative (using standard factor):**
- Annuity Factor at 18% for 4 years = 2.6901
$$PV_{\text{Annuity, A}} = 500,000 \times 2.6901 = \text{Rs. } 1,345,050$$

### Step 2: Calculate Present Worth

$$PW_A = -\text{Initial Investment} + PV_{\text{Annuity}}$$

$$PW_A = -1,500,000 + 1,345,050$$

$$\boxed{PW_A = \text{Rs. } -154,950}$$

**Interpretation:** The present worth is negative, meaning the revenues don't fully recover the initial investment at 18% interest rate.

---

## MACHINE B Analysis

### Step 1: Calculate PV of Annual Revenues

$$PV_{\text{Annuity, B}} = 800,000 \times 2.6901 = \text{Rs. } 2,152,080$$

### Step 2: Calculate Present Worth

$$PW_B = -2,200,000 + 2,152,080$$

$$\boxed{PW_B = \text{Rs. } -47,920}$$

---

## Comparison & Recommendation

| Machine | Initial Investment | PV of Revenues | Present Worth |
|---------|---|---|---|
| **A** | (1,500,000) | 1,345,050 | **-154,950** |
| **B** | (2,200,000) | 2,152,080 | **-47,920** |

$$\boxed{\text{MACHINE B SHOULD BE SELECTED}}$$

**Advantage of B over A:**
$$\text{Better PW} = |-47,920| - |-154,950| = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 107,030 \text{ advantage}}$$

Or expressed differently:
$$\text{Better by} = -47,920 - (-154,950) = \boxed{\text{Rs. } 107,030}$$

**Justification:**
- Although both machines have negative present worth at 18% interest rate (meaning neither fully recovers investment at this high rate)
- **Machine B is the LESS negative option** - it performs relatively better
- Machine B recovers 97.8% of its investment vs Machine A recovering only 89.7%
- The higher annual revenue of Machine B (Rs. 800,000 vs Rs. 500,000) more than compensates for the higher initial investment
- **Machine B is the better choice with Rs. 107,030 economic advantage in present worth terms**

## Part (b): Scholarship Fund - Present Value of Annuity [4 Marks]

### Given Information

- **Annual Scholarship Payout:** Rs. 200,000
- **Duration:** 15 years
- **Annual Interest Rate:** 12%
- **Required:** Find lump-sum amount to invest today (Present Value)

---

### Solution: Present Value of Ordinary Annuity

This is finding the **Present Value of an Ordinary Annuity** (payments at end of each period).

**Formula:**
$$PV = \frac{A \times [(1+i)^n - 1]}{i(1+i)^n}$$

Or using factor:
$$PV = A \times \text{PV Annuity Factor}$$

Where:
- A = Annual payment = Rs. 200,000
- i = Interest rate = 12% = 0.12
- n = Number of years = 15

---

### Step 1: Calculate (1.12)¹⁵

$$(1.12)^{15} = 5.4735$$

---

### Step 2: Calculate PV Factor

$$\text{PV Factor} = \frac{(1.12)^{15} - 1}{0.12 \times (1.12)^{15}}$$

$$\text{PV Factor} = \frac{5.4735 - 1}{0.12 \times 5.4735} = \frac{4.4735}{0.6568}$$

$$\text{PV Factor} = 6.8109$$

**Or using standard annuity table:**
- PV Annuity Factor at 12% for 15 years = **6.8109**

---

### Step 3: Calculate Present Value

$$PV = A \times \text{PV Factor}$$

$$PV = 200,000 \times 6.8109$$

$$\boxed{PV = \text{Rs. } 13,62,180}$$

---

### Verification

If the foundation invests **Rs. 13,62,180 today** at 12% annual interest:

| Year | Beginning Balance | Interest @ 12% | Withdrawal | Ending Balance |
|------|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 13,62,180 | 163,462 | (200,000) | 13,25,642 |
| 2 | 13,25,642 | 159,077 | (200,000) | 12,84,719 |
| 3 | 12,84,719 | 154,166 | (200,000) | 12,38,885 |
| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| 15 | ~200,000 | ~24,000 | (200,000) | ~0 |

✓ After 15 years, the fund depletes to approximately zero.

---

### Alternative Calculation (Verification)

Using direct formula:
$$PV = \frac{200,000 \times [(1.12)^{15} - 1]}{0.12 \times (1.12)^{15}}$$

$$PV = \frac{200,000 \times 4.4735}{0.6568} = \frac{894,700}{0.6568}$$

$$PV = \text{Rs. } 13,62,180$$ ✓

---

### Answer

$$\boxed{\text{The foundation should invest Rs. 13,62,180 today}}$$

**Interpretation:**
- This lump-sum amount, when invested at 12% annual interest, will generate sufficient funds to pay out Rs. 200,000 every year for 15 years
- The interest earned each year supplements the principal to maintain the annual payout
- By the end of Year 15, the fund will be completely depleted after the final scholarship payment
