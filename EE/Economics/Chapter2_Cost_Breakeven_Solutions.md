# Chapter 2: Cost Elements & Breakeven Analysis

## 💵 1. Cost Classification

### A. Variable Costs
**Definition:** Costs that **vary** with production volume

| Type | Full Form | Examples |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **Direct Material Cost** | Cost of raw materials | Steel, plastic, fabric, components |
| **Direct Labour Cost** | Wages for production workers | Assembly line wages, machine operators |
| **Direct Expenses** | Purchase-related costs | Freight, insurance, carriage, import duty |

### B. Overhead Costs (Fixed Costs)
**Definition:** Costs that remain **constant** regardless of production volume

| Type | Full Form | Examples |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **Administration Overhead** | Management costs | Legal fees, audit fees, office rent, salaries |
| **Selling Overhead** | Marketing & sales costs | Advertising, commission, showroom expenses |
| **Distribution Overhead** | Delivery costs | Packing, transportation, warehouse rent |

---

## 📊 2. Basic Revenue and Cost Formulas

### Total Sales Revenue (S or TR)

$$\boxed{S = s \times Q}$$

**Variable Definitions:**
| Symbol | Full Form | Unit | Description |
|--------|-----------|------|-------------|
| S (or TR) | Total Sales/Total Revenue | Rs., $ | Total income from sales |
| s | Selling price per unit | Rs./unit, $/unit | Price at which each unit is sold |
| Q | Quantity | units | Number of units produced/sold |

**When to Use:** Calculating total sales income at any output level

**Example:** *"Selling price = $50/unit, Quantity sold = 100 units. Find total sales."*
```
S = 50 × 100 = $5,000
```

---

### Total Cost (TC)

$$\boxed{TC = TVC + TFC}$$
$$\boxed{TC = (v \times Q) + FC}$$

**Variable Definitions:**
| Symbol | Full Form | Unit | Description |
|--------|-----------|------|-------------|
| TC | Total Cost | Rs., $ | All costs combined |
| TVC | Total Variable Cost | Rs., $ | All variable costs |
| TFC (or FC) | Total Fixed Cost / Fixed Cost | Rs., $ | All fixed costs |
| v | Variable cost per unit | Rs./unit, $/unit | Cost per unit that varies with Q |
| Q | Quantity | units | Production volume |

**When to Use:** Finding total production cost at any output level

**Example:** *"Variable cost = $30/unit, Fixed cost = $500, Quantity = 25 units. Find total cost."*
```
TC = (30 × 25) + 500 = 750 + 500 = $1,250
```

---

### Profit

$$\boxed{\text{Profit} = S - TC}$$
$$\boxed{\text{Profit} = (s \times Q) - (FC + v \times Q)}$$
$$\boxed{\text{Profit} = Q(s - v) - FC}$$

**Variable Definitions:**
| Symbol | Full Form | Description |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Profit | Net Profit/Loss | Positive = Profit, Negative = Loss, Zero = Breakeven |

**When to Use:** Finding profit/loss at any production level

**Example:** *"s = $50, v = $30, FC = $500, Q = 30. Find profit."*
```
Profit = 30(50 - 30) - 500 = 30(20) - 500 = 600 - 500 = $100 ✓
```

---

## 🎯 3. Breakeven Quantity (BEQ) ⭐⭐⭐

### Formula:
$$\boxed{BEQ = \frac{FC}{s - v}}$$

**Variable Definitions:**
| Symbol | Full Form | Unit | Description |
|--------|-----------|------|-------------|
| BEQ | Breakeven Quantity | units | Units needed to breakeven |
| FC | Fixed Cost | Rs., $ | Total fixed costs |
| s | Selling price per unit | Rs./unit, $/unit | Price per unit sold |
| v | Variable cost per unit | Rs./unit, $/unit | Variable cost per unit |
| (s - v) | Contribution per unit | Rs./unit, $/unit | Money per unit for fixed costs |

**When to Use:**
✓ Finding minimum units to cover all costs  
✓ Determining production targets  
✓ Analyzing whether business is viable  
✓ **MOST IMPORTANT FORMULA** - appears in almost every cost problem

**Derivation:**
```
At breakeven: Profit = 0
Q(s - v) - FC = 0
Q(s - v) = FC
Q = FC / (s - v)  ✓
```

**Example Problem:**
**Statement:** *"A firm has fixed costs of $500. Selling price is $50 per unit and variable cost is $30 per unit. Find breakeven quantity."*

**Given:**
- FC = $500
- s = $50/unit
- v = $30/unit

**Solution:**
$$BEQ = \frac{500}{50 - 30} = \frac{500}{20} = \boxed{25 \text{ units}}$$

**Verification:**
```
At Q = 25:
Revenue = 50 × 25 = $1,250
Total Cost = (30 × 25) + 500 = $1,250
Profit = 1,250 - 1,250 = $0 ✓ (Breakeven confirmed)
```

---

## 💲 4. Breakeven Sales

### Formula:
$$\boxed{\text{Breakeven Sales} = \frac{FC \times s}{s - v}}$$

**OR**

$$\boxed{\text{Breakeven Sales} = BEQ \times s}$$

**Variable Definitions:**
| Symbol | Full Form | Unit | Description |
|--------|-----------|------|-------------|
| Breakeven Sales | Breakeven Sales Revenue | Rs., $ | Sales revenue at breakeven point |

**When to Use:** Finding sales revenue (not units) at breakeven

**Example:** Using previous example (BEQ = 25 units, s = $50)
```
Breakeven Sales = 25 × 50 = $1,250
OR
Breakeven Sales = (500 × 50)/(50 - 30) = 25,000/20 = $1,250 ✓
```

---

## 🔧 5. Contribution

### Formula:
$$\boxed{\text{Contribution per unit} = s - v}$$
$$\boxed{\text{Total Contribution} = Q(s - v)}$$

**Variable Definitions:**
| Symbol | Full Form | Description |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Contribution | Contribution Margin | Amount available to cover FC and profit |

**When to Use:**
✓ Building block for BEQ formula  
✓ Analyzing profitability per unit  
✓ Calculating P/V ratio

**Example:** *"s = $50, v = $30. Find contribution."*
```
Contribution per unit = 50 - 30 = $20
```
**Interpretation:** Each unit contributes $20 towards fixed costs and profit

---

## 🛡️ 6. Margin of Safety

### Formula:
$$\boxed{\text{Margin of Safety} = \text{Actual Sales} - \text{Breakeven Sales}}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Margin of Safety \%} = \frac{\text{Actual Sales} - \text{BE Sales}}{\text{Actual Sales}} \times 100}$$

**Variable Definitions:**
| Symbol | Full Form | Description |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Margin of Safety | Safety Buffer | Sales cushion above breakeven |
| Actual Sales | Current Sales Revenue | Actual sales in Rs./$ |
| BE Sales | Breakeven Sales | Sales needed to breakeven |

**When to Use:**
✓ Measuring risk (how far sales can drop before loss)  
✓ Business safety assessment  
✓ Comparing investment alternatives

**Example:** *"Actual sales = $2,500, Breakeven sales = $1,250. Find margin of safety."*
```
Margin of Safety = 2,500 - 1,250 = $1,250

Margin of Safety % = (1,250/2,500) × 100 = 50%
```
**Interpretation:** Sales can drop by 50% before business reaches breakeven

---

## 📈 7. P/V Ratio (Profit/Volume Ratio) ⭐⭐

### Formulas:
$$\boxed{\text{P/V Ratio} = \frac{\text{Contribution}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100}$$

$$\boxed{\text{P/V Ratio} = \frac{s - v}{s} \times 100}$$

$$\boxed{\text{P/V Ratio} = \frac{\Delta \text{Profit}}{\Delta \text{Sales}} \times 100}$$

**Variable Definitions:**
| Symbol | Full Form | Unit | Description |
|--------|-----------|------|-------------|
| P/V Ratio | Profit-Volume Ratio | % | Contribution per rupee/dollar of sales |
| Δ Profit | Change in Profit | Rs., $ | Difference in profit levels |
| Δ Sales | Change in Sales | Rs., $ | Difference in sales levels |

**When to Use:**
✓ Finding contribution per rupee/dollar of sales  
✓ Calculating profit at different sales levels  
✓ Alternative method for breakeven calculations  
✓ Comparing profitability of products

**Example:** *"s = $50, v = $30. Find P/V ratio."*
```
P/V Ratio = [(50 - 30)/50] × 100 = (20/50) × 100 = 40%
```
**Interpretation:** Every $1 of sales contributes $0.40 towards fixed costs and profit

---

### Related P/V Formulas:

**Profit using P/V Ratio:**
$$\boxed{\text{Profit} = (\text{Sales} \times \text{P/V Ratio}) - FC}$$

**Required Sales for Target Profit:**
$$\boxed{\text{Required Sales} = \frac{FC + \text{Desired Profit}}{\text{P/V Ratio}}}$$

**Example:** *"FC = $500, P/V Ratio = 40%, Desired profit = $300. Find required sales."*
```
Required Sales = (500 + 300) / 0.40 = 800 / 0.40 = $2,000
```

## 📋 8. COMPLETE EXAMPLE: Breakeven Table Analysis

### Problem Statement:
*"A firm produces units with the following cost structure. Selling price is $50 per unit. Find: (a) Total cost and sales at each output level, (b) Profit at each level, (c) Breakeven point, (d) Marginal Revenue, (e) Marginal Cost."*

### Given Data:

| Output (Q) | TVC ($) | TFC ($) |
|------------|---------|---------|
| 0          | 0       | 500     |
| 10         | 300     | 500     |
| 20         | 600     | 500     |
| 25         | 750     | 500     |
| 30         | 900     | 500     |
| 40         | 1200    | 500     |
| 50         | 1500    | 500     |

**Where:**
- **Q** = Quantity/Output (units)
- **TVC** = Total Variable Cost ($)
- **TFC** = Total Fixed Cost ($)
- **s** = Selling price = $50/unit

---

### Solution:

#### Step 1: Calculate Total Cost (TC)

$$TC = TVC + TFC$$

| Q  | TVC  | TFC | TC = TVC + TFC |
|----|------|-----|----------------|
| 0  | 0    | 500 | **500**        |
| 10 | 300  | 500 | **800**        |
| 20 | 600  | 500 | **1100**       |
| 25 | 750  | 500 | **1250**       |
| 30 | 900  | 500 | **1400**       |
| 40 | 1200 | 500 | **1700**       |
| 50 | 1500 | 500 | **2000**       |

---

#### Step 2: Calculate Total Sales (S)

$$S = s \times Q = 50 \times Q$$

| Q  | S = 50 × Q |
|----|------------|
| 0  | **0**      |
| 10 | **500**    |
| 20 | **1000**   |
| 25 | **1250**   |
| 30 | **1500**   |
| 40 | **2000**   |
| 50 | **2500**   |

---

#### Step 3: Calculate Profit

$$\text{Profit} = S - TC$$

| Q  | S    | TC   | Profit = S - TC | Status |
|----|------|------|-----------------|---------|
| 0  | 0    | 500  | **-500**        | Loss    |
| 10 | 500  | 800  | **-300**        | Loss    |
| 20 | 1000 | 1100 | **-100**        | Loss    |
| 25 | 1250 | 1250 | **0**           | ✅ **BREAKEVEN** |
| 30 | 1500 | 1400 | **+100**        | Profit  |
| 40 | 2000 | 1700 | **+300**        | Profit  |
| 50 | 2500 | 2000 | **+500**        | Profit  |

**Breakeven Point:** Q = 25 units (Profit = 0)

---

#### Step 4: Calculate Variable Cost per Unit (v)

$$v = \frac{TVC}{Q} = \frac{1500}{50} = \$30 \text{ per unit}$$

**Verification:** At Q = 10: v = 300/10 = $30 ✓ (Constant)

---

#### Step 5: Verify BEQ using Formula

$$BEQ = \frac{FC}{s - v} = \frac{500}{50 - 30} = \frac{500}{20} = \boxed{25 \text{ units}} \checkmark$$

**Matches table result!**

---

#### Step 6: Calculate Marginal Revenue (MR)

$$MR = \frac{\Delta S}{\Delta Q} = \frac{\text{Change in Sales}}{\text{Change in Quantity}}$$

For **linear pricing** (constant price):
$$MR = s = \$50 \text{ per unit}$$

**Why?** Each additional unit sells for $50

---

#### Step 7: Calculate Marginal Cost (MC)

$$MC = \frac{\Delta TC}{\Delta Q}$$

| Q₁ → Q₂ | ΔTC = TC₂ - TC₁ | ΔQ = Q₂ - Q₁ | MC = ΔTC/ΔQ |
|---------|-----------------|--------------|-------------|
| 0 → 10  | 800 - 500 = 300 | 10          | **30**      |
| 10 → 20 | 1100 - 800 = 300| 10          | **30**      |
| 20 → 25 | 1250 - 1100 = 150| 5          | **30**      |
| 25 → 30 | 1400 - 1250 = 150| 5          | **30**      |
| 30 → 40 | 1700 - 1400 = 300| 10         | **30**      |
| 40 → 50 | 2000 - 1700 = 300| 10         | **30**      |

**MC = $30 per unit** (constant, equals v)

**Why constant?** Because variable cost per unit is constant

---

#### Step 8: Calculate P/V Ratio

$$\text{P/V Ratio} = \frac{s - v}{s} \times 100 = \frac{50 - 30}{50} \times 100 = \frac{20}{50} \times 100 = \boxed{40\%}$$

**Interpretation:** Each $1 of sales contributes $0.40 to fixed costs and profit

---

#### Step 9: Calculate Margin of Safety (at Q = 50)

$$\text{Actual Sales} = \$2500$$
$$\text{BE Sales} = \$1250$$
$$\text{Margin of Safety} = 2500 - 1250 = \boxed{\$1250}$$

$$\text{Margin of Safety \%} = \frac{1250}{2500} \times 100 = \boxed{50\%}$$

**Interpretation:** Sales can drop 50% before reaching breakeven

---

### Summary of Results:

| Metric | Value | Formula Used |
|--------|-------|--------------|
| **Breakeven Quantity** | 25 units | FC/(s-v) |
| **Breakeven Sales** | $1,250 | BEQ × s |
| **Variable Cost per unit** | $30 | TVC/Q |
| **Contribution per unit** | $20 | s - v |
| **Marginal Revenue** | $50/unit | s (constant price) |
| **Marginal Cost** | $30/unit | ΔTC/ΔQ |
| **P/V Ratio** | 40% | (s-v)/s × 100 |
| **Margin of Safety** (Q=50) | $1,250 (50%) | Actual Sales - BE Sales |

---

## 🎯 Key Takeaways:

1. **Below BEQ (Q < 25):** Loss  
2. **At BEQ (Q = 25):** Zero profit (breakeven)  
3. **Above BEQ (Q > 25):** Profit increases by $20 per unit (contribution)
4. **MR > MC:** $50 > $30, so producing more units increases profit  
5. **P/V Ratio = 40%:** Every additional $1 sales adds $0.40 profit (after breakeven)
